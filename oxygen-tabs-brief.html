Tabs

One control everybody thinks they already have. In practice it is four different components sharing a silhouette — a view switch, a filter, a navigation menu and a wizard — and the reason tabs are the most-reported accessibility defect in every design system audit is that teams ship one of them and use it as all four. This is the tab system Oxygen ships instead: eleven visual variants over four semantic modes, one indicator engine, one token surface, and an overflow strategy that survives twenty-three tabs on a 360 px phone.

01

Four components wearing one coat

The screenshot that started this — a two-up pill switch reading Personal / Shared — is the single most requested control in the product, and it is also the one most likely to be built wrong, because its appearance says nothing about what it is. Look at four real screens and the same silhouette carries four incompatible contracts:

Mode 1 · tabs

A view switch

Panels of the same object. role="tablist", arrow keys move selection, Tab leaves the group entirely. Exactly one panel visible.

Patient chart: Summary · Vitals · Labs · Notes

Mode 2 · nav

Navigation

Each item is a URL and a document load. Must be <nav><a> with aria-current="page" — never a tablist. Users expect middle-click, cmd-click, copy-link, back button.

Settings: Account · Billing · Team

Mode 3 · radiogroup

A value, not a view

The segmented control that filters a list is a form input. It is role="radiogroup", it belongs inside Form.Item, and it submits. Calling it a tab makes it unlabelable and unvalidatable.

Chart range: 1D · 1W · 1M · 1Y

Mode 4 · steps

A sequence

Order is meaningful, later steps may be unreachable, and leaving one may need to save. Selection is gated by onBeforeChange, not free.

Intake: Identity → Insurance → Consent → Review

The defect this design exists to prevent

A role="tablist" wrapped around anchors that change the URL is the most common tab bug in production. The screen reader announces “tab, 2 of 5”, the user presses → expecting to preview the next panel, and instead the page navigates and their focus is destroyed. It passes every automated checker, because every individual attribute is spelled correctly.

So the primary prop in this component is not variant. It is as — the semantic mode — and it is required. The visual variant is orthogonal and free: any of the eleven skins can dress any of the four modes, because the skin is CSS and the mode is the accessibility tree.

The whole thesis in one signature
// Two independent axes. Neither defaults to the other.
<Tabs
  as="tabs" | "nav" | "radiogroup" | "steps"   // required — the a11y tree
  variant="segmented" | "underline" | "pill" | "enclosed"
         | "rail" | "ghost" | "stepper" | "command"
         | "card" | "stat" | "unstyled"              // free — the skin
/>

Anatomy of the flagship

The reference build, rendered at 1:1 against the source screenshot. Every measurement below is a token, not a magic number — which is what makes the same component survive three themes and three densities without a fork.

V01 Segmented variant="segmented" as="tabs" tablist
Personal — 14 documents only you can see. panel id=an-p1
Shared — 6 documents shared with your care team. panel id=an-p2
1 2 3 4 5 6
Click either half, or focus it and press ←/→. The thumb is a single transformed element — not a moving background — so it interpolates position and width together and never repaints the track.
  • 1
    Track — --ox-tabs-track-bg, --ox-tabs-track-border, --ox-tabs-track-radius. The 1 px border is what keeps the control legible on a card that is also grey; without it the segmented control disappears on --ox-bg-subtle.
  • 2
    Track inset — --ox-tabs-track-pad, 4 px at standard density, 3 px clinical, 5 px patient. This is the gap the thumb never crosses and the reason thumb radius is smaller than track radius: concentric radii, outer − pad = inner.
  • 3
    Thumb — one absolutely-positioned element driven by --ind-x/--ind-w. Carries --ox-tabs-thumb-bg, a hairline border and a two-layer shadow. aria-hidden: it is decoration, the state lives on aria-selected.
  • 4
    Unselected label — --ox-tabs-fg at weight 450. Deliberately not a lighter grey than 4.5:1; an unselected tab is still an operable control and must meet contrast, which rules out most of the segmented controls shipping today.
  • 5
    Hairline separator — a ::before between adjacent unselected triggers, faded out around the thumb and on hover. At two tabs you barely notice it; at five it is the difference between a control and a smear.
  • 6
    Hit target — --ox-tabs-min-h resolves from --ox-density-target: 44 px standard, 48 px patient, 30 px clinical. Clinical is below WCAG 2.5.8's 24 px minimum only in spacing, never in size, and is opt-in per surface.
Why weight changes but the box does not

Selected labels move from 450 to 550. Naïvely that reflows the track and the thumb chases a moving target. The trigger therefore reserves the bold width up front with a ::after carrying the same text at the selected weight in visibility:hidden — so the box is stable and the thumb animates to a position that will still be correct when the transition ends.

03 · Equal fill — the mobile default

Two equal halves. Grid, not flex — grid-auto-columns:1fr makes each column the width of the widest label so the thumb never resizes as you switch.
Rule: fill="equal" at ≤2 items on ≤480 px. Above 4 items equal fill produces 60 px cells and truncated labels — switch to overflow="scroll".
V03bfill="equal" · 4-upthe failure it prevents
Four short labels is the ceiling. The moment one reads “Last 90 days”, equal fill is the wrong tool.

04 · Sizes

V04size="sm" · "md" · "lg"independent of density
Size ≠ density. Density is a property of the surface and set once on a container; size is a property of this instance and overrides it. A clinical-density page can still host one size="lg" control on its primary action. Omit size and the component inherits density — which is what you want 90 % of the time.

07 · Pill filters

V07Pillvariant="pill" as="radiogroup"wrapsradiogroup
Wrapping is the point. Pills are the only variant that reflows to multiple rows, which is why they are the right choice for a filter set of unknown length and the wrong choice for a tab strip — a two-row tablist gives you no stable “next tab” direction for →. There is no indicator element: with wrapping, a sliding thumb would have to animate diagonally across a line break.

08 · Enclosed & editable

V08Enclosedvariant="enclosed" editableclosableaddabledirty
Progress note — draft, last autosave 40 s ago. dirty=true

The close button is not a button. A nested interactive element inside a role="tab" is invalid — screen readers flatten it or skip it. Oxygen renders it as role="button" with tabindex="-1" and binds Delete/Backspace on the tab itself, which is what APG actually prescribes for a closable tab. After a close, focus moves to the neighbour deterministically (next, else previous, else the add button) — never to <body>.

05

Overflow — the part everyone ships broken

Tabs are easy until there are eleven of them in 340 px. Every strategy below is a real trade-off, none dominates, and the component therefore exposes the choice rather than picking for you. What it will not do is the industry default — let the strip overflow the viewport and hope.

O1 · overflow="scroll" — scroll with affordances

O1Scrollfade masks + nudge buttons + snapdefault
Arrow-key to the last tab and watch it scroll itself into view with scrollIntoView({block:'nearest', inline:'nearest'}) — never center, which yanks the strip on every keypress.
The nudge buttons are tabindex="-1". They duplicate a capability the keyboard already has (arrow keys move selection and scroll follows), so putting them in the tab order adds two stops that do nothing for a keyboard user. They exist for trackpad-less mice and touch users who cannot see the strip is scrollable. Fades are CSS masks over the container, not gradients over the track, so they work on any background including the dark theme.

O2 · overflow="menu" — priority-plus

O2More menuResizeObserver + measured fitdesktop dense
    Resize this window. Tabs that no longer fit move into the menu, and the button shows the overflow count. Selecting from the menu promotes that tab to the front of the visible set so the current tab is always visible.
    Measured, not guessed. Widths come from one ResizeObserver on the list plus a cached per-tab width map, recomputed on font load (document.fonts.ready) and on locale change. The naïve implementation — hide with CSS and count — reports zero widths for hidden elements and collapses the whole strip. The overflow menu is a real role="menu" with its own key handling, not a second tablist; a tablist split across two containers has an incoherent “N of M”.
    O3collapseoverflow="collapse"
    Below a container-query breakpoint, the entire tablist becomes a native <select>.
    Container query, not media query. Tabs inside a 380 px drawer on a 1440 px monitor need the same treatment as tabs on a phone; @container is the only correct trigger.
    O4wrapoverflow="wrap" · pill only
    Only legal for variant="pill" in radiogroup mode. The component throws in dev if you ask a tablist to wrap: two-dimensional arrow navigation in a one-dimensional widget has no correct answer.

    06

    States and edge cases

    Oxygen's design contract requires seven states to have a defined design rather than a default. Tabs have an eighth: the panel that will not let you leave. Every one of these is a designed state in the package, not something the consuming team invents at 4 pm.

    S1Loading — strip
    Skeleton the strip only when the tab set is unknown. If you know the labels, render them and skeleton the panel — a strip that appears late shifts the whole page and costs you CLS.
    S2Loading — panel (lazy)
    Loading imaging study…
    aria-busy="true" · polite live region announces on settle
    The panel keeps its box. min-height is pinned to the last measured height of that panel so switching back and forth does not bounce the page.
    S3Empty
    Nothing shared with you yet When a colleague shares a record it appears here.
    An empty tab is never hidden. Removing it changes the tab count between sessions and destroys muscle memory. Show it, show the zero, explain the emptiness.
    S4Error in one panel
    Could not load chemistry results. The lab interface returned 503.
    One failed panel does not fail the strip. Each panel gets its own error boundary; the failing tab carries a status dot so the failure is visible from the tab you are on.
    S5Restricted recordbreak-the-glass
    Behavioural health is restricted. Opening it records an access event against your account and notifies the record owner.
    Present, locked, and explained. Hiding a restricted section teaches clinicians the record is incomplete, which is a worse failure than telling them it exists and is gated.
    S6Unsaved-changes guardinteractive
    Leave without saving?

    Your note has unsaved changes. Switching tabs will discard them.

    Switch away from Compose to trigger the guard.
    onBeforeChange may return a promise. While it is pending the tab shows a busy state and the strip is inert — the one case where a tab is legitimately unresponsive. Focus returns to the tab you tried to leave if you cancel.
    Offline and degraded

    Oxygen's contract lists offline as its own state, and tabs make it awkward: the strip renders from cache, panels do not. The component exposes items[].availability = "ready" | "stale" | "unavailable", which paints a subdued dot on the trigger and lets the panel decide whether to show cached content with a staleness banner or refuse. It does not disable the tab — a clinician looking at stale vitals needs to know they are stale, not be told the section does not exist.

    Roving tabindex

    One stop, not eleven

    Exactly one trigger holds tabindex="0"; the rest are -1. A Tab press from anywhere above lands on the current tab, and the next Tab lands in the panel. aria-activedescendant is deliberately not used — it loses real DOM focus, and real focus is what carries the focus ring on Windows High Contrast.

    Automatic vs manual

    When arrowing should load

    activation="automatic" (default) selects as you arrow — correct when panels are cheap. activation="manual" moves focus only and waits for Enter — required when a panel triggers a network request, because automatic activation across six tabs fires six requests and reads six live regions.

    Panel focus

    tabindex="0", conditionally

    A panel with no focusable content gets tabindex="0" so it can be reached and scrolled. A panel that has focusable content must not — an extra stop before the first control is noise. The component checks at mount and on content change.

    Announcements

    Silence is the goal

    Switching tabs does not announce anything beyond the tab's own name and state; the panel is not a live region. Only asynchronous arrivals — a lazy panel settling, a guard rejecting — post to a polite live region, and never more than once per action.

    Close focus order

    Deterministic, not body

    After closing tab n: focus tab n (which is now the old n+1), else n−1, else the add button, else the tablist container. This is the rule APG omits and the reason closable tabs feel broken in most implementations.

    Windows High Contrast

    Selection survives the palette wipe

    In forced-colors mode the thumb's background and shadow are erased by the OS. The component adds forced-color-adjust rules that give the selected trigger a Highlight border and an underline — because if selection is only a fill, in HCM there is no selection.

    Forced colors — the block most libraries omit
    @media (forced-colors: active) {
  .ox-tab[aria-selected="true"],
  .ox-tab[aria-checked="true"] {
    forced-color-adjust: none;
    background: Highlight;
    color: HighlightText;
  }
  .ox-thumb { display: none; }              /* the OS erased it anyway */
  .ox-line  { background: Highlight; forced-color-adjust: none; }
  .ox-tab:focus-visible { outline: 2px solid CanvasText; }
}
    08

    The indicator engine

    Every variant that has a moving indicator shares one implementation, roughly 90 lines. It is the part that looks trivial and is not: the naïve version — animate left and width on a positioned div — repaints the strip on every frame, tears at fractional device pixels, and lands in the wrong place whenever a web font arrives after hydration.

    The measurement pass
    // One element. Two custom properties. Zero layout thrash.
const place = (list, el) => {
  const l = list.getBoundingClientRect();
  const t = el.getBoundingClientRect();
  // Read every rect first, then write — never interleave.
  list.style.setProperty("--ind-x", `${t.left - l.left + list.scrollLeft}px`);
  list.style.setProperty("--ind-w", `${t.width}px`);
  list.style.setProperty("--ind-y", `${t.top  - l.top  + list.scrollTop}px`);
  list.style.setProperty("--ind-h", `${t.height}px`);
  list.dataset.measured = "";   // reveals the indicator, hides the CSS fallback
};
    Re-measure triggers

    Six of them, all real

    Selection change · ResizeObserver on the list · ResizeObserver on each trigger (label text can change without the list resizing) · document.fonts.ready · locale change · the strip scrolling. Miss the font one and the indicator is 12 px off for the first 300 ms of every cold load.

    Transform, not offset

    Composited both ways

    translate3d for position; width is the one non-composited property and is unavoidable if the indicator is to match label widths. It is one element on its own layer, so the cost is a single 2 ms layout per transition rather than a repaint of the strip.

    SSR and first paint

    No naked flash

    Before JS measures, .ox-list:not([data-measured]) hides the indicator and paints selection directly on the selected trigger with a box-shadow that mimics the thumb. The server-rendered strip therefore looks correct with JavaScript disabled, and hydration swaps to the animated indicator without a visible jump.

    Reduced motion

    Instant, not absent

    prefers-reduced-motion collapses the duration to 1 ms rather than removing the transition. Removing it entirely means the transitionend housekeeping never fires; every library that has shipped this bug ends up with a stuck will-change layer.

    RTL

    Logical properties throughout

    inset-inline-start, not left. The measurement is delta-based so it is direction-agnostic, and the only RTL-specific code is the arrow-key mapping, which reads getComputedStyle(list).direction rather than assuming. Flip the Dir control above and watch every demo on this page work.

    Optional: View Transitions

    Off by default

    transition="view" uses startViewTransition to cross-fade panel content alongside the indicator. It is opt-in because it serialises the update, which is wrong for a strip you can arrow through quickly, and unsupported in Firefox as of this writing — the fallback is the standard transition.

    Measured cost

    Eleven tabs, cold: 1 measurement pass (0.4 ms on an M1, 1.9 ms on a mid-range Android), 2 ResizeObserver registrations, 0 layout thrash — reads and writes are batched through one requestAnimationFrame. Switching tabs: no measurement at all unless a resize fired, because per-trigger rects are cached and invalidated by the observers rather than recomputed.

    Ant Design interop

    Oxygen wraps antd rather than replacing it, and antd already has Tabs and Segmented. The honest position:

    • antd Tabs covers as="tabs" with line/card types well. Where Oxygen adds value is the other three modes, the eleven skins, the overflow choice, and the clinical states — none of which antd models.
    • antd Segmented is role="radiogroup" internally and is a reasonable base for as="radiogroup". Oxygen's version exists because Segmented has no icon+count+tone trigger, no overflow story and no density axis.
    • Theme — Oxygen reads antd's ConfigProvider theme and maps colorPrimary, borderRadius and controlHeight onto the --ox-tabs-* tokens, so a host that has already branded antd gets a tab strip that matches without touching Oxygen's tokens.

    The open question flagged in the library proposal — whether Oxygen components should be antd-derived or registry-only — applies here and is not settled by this brief. This design is implementable either way; the compound layer is antd-free, and only the ConfigProvider bridge would be dropped.

    Performance, SSR and testing

    Bundle

    Budget: 4.2 kB gzipped

    tabs-core 2.1 kB (no React DOM, no antd) + tabs 2.1 kB. Variants are CSS; importing one skin does not pull the others' JavaScript because there is none. The overflow menu and the drag-reorder handler are dynamic imports, loaded only when overflow="menu" or editable.onReorder is set.

    RSC

    Server component by default

    Tabs.List and Tabs.Trigger render on the server. Only the interaction layer is "use client". A as="nav" strip is fully static — zero client JS unless you ask for the animated indicator.

    Many tabs

    Virtualisation, reluctantly

    Above ~120 triggers the per-trigger ResizeObserver becomes the cost centre and virtualise switches to a windowed strip. It is off by default and documented as a smell: 120 tabs is a navigation problem, not a rendering one.

    Testing

    Behaviour, not snapshots

    14

    What this component will not do

    Stated plainly, because an unstated boundary becomes someone's bug report.

    • It will not render a tablist of links. Passing href with as="tabs" is a lint error and, in dev, a thrown exception.
    • It will not wrap a tablist onto two rows. Use overflow="menu" or "collapse".
    • It will not hide a disabled or empty tab. Both change the tab count between sessions.
    • It will not reorder tabs by importance. Position is memory.
    • It will not animate panel height. A height transition on arbitrary content is a jank generator and an accessibility problem; panels swap, they do not grow.
    • It will not own routing. syncTo writes to a URL through an adapter you supply. Oxygen does not depend on a router.
    • It will not enforce your permissions. disabled + availability are presentation. Authorisation is the host's, on the server, and a hidden panel is not a security control.
    • It is not a compliance boundary. Same as everything else in Oxygen: it does not make an application HIPAA, GDPR or DPDP compliant, and it is not a medical device or clinical decision support.

    Built — and what building it changed

    Shipped as @oxygenui-design/tabs-core and @oxygenui-design/tabs, plus the @oxygenui/tabs-semantic-mode lint rule. Five things in this brief were wrong, and the implementation corrected them rather than the other way round:

    • §04 V08 claimed the close button should be role="button" with tabindex="-1". It is still a nested interactive control inside role="tab", and axe flags it — correctly. The shipped affordance is aria-hidden with a pointer handler; the keyboard path is Delete on the tab, which is what APG actually prescribes.
    • The add button and the More trigger were drawn inside the tablist. role="tablist" may own only role="tab"; anything else fails aria-required-children and corrupts the announced “n of m”. They are now siblings, in a .ox-tabs__bar wrapper.
    • Collapsing to a <select> by swapping the rendered tree remounted every trigger, which churned the registry, which re-ran the measurement that decides whether to collapse — an infinite loop reachable from an ordinary narrow container. The tablist now stays mounted and is hidden.
    • aria-controls was emitted unconditionally, so a strip used without panels pointed at an element that did not exist.
    • Typeahead did not implement APG's repeat-to-cycle rule, so pressing L three times searched for “lll” and stranded the user on the first L tab.
    Open questions this brief does not close

    1. antd-derived or registry-only — the library-wide call, unchanged by this design. The shipped package takes the third road: antd is an optional peer, the stylesheet falls through to --ant-* with no JavaScript, and the bridge lives behind a separate ./antd entry point. 2. Whether as should default to "tabs" for adoption ease; this brief argues no, it shipped as required, and that argument is still worth one round of pushback. 3. Whether stat and card belong in Tabs at all or are separate components that happen to share the selection core. They shipped as variants.
