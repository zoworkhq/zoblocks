Oxygen UI · Component brief · 16 August 2026 · implemented

An accordion decides what a clinician does not see.

In every other product category, a collapsed section hides convenience. In behavioral health it hides a suicide-risk item, a safety plan, a note the patient has a legal right to read, or a substance-use record governed by a different federal rule than the chart around it. The generic accordion assumes hidden means unneeded. That assumption is what this component has to replace.

What changed once it was built

This brief was written first and the component was then implemented against it. Four claims did not survive contact and have been corrected in place, each marked ↻ revised where it appears:

  • React cannot render hidden="until-found". React 19 treats hidden as a boolean attribute and serialises the string to hidden="" — verified against react-dom 19.2 in both renderToString and the client. §04.4 said the attribute was rendered directly; it is now rendered as a boolean and upgraded after commit, which is also what keeps the server-rendered markup correct.
  • No feature detection is needed, and the brief was wrong to call for it. hidden="until-found" degrades on its own: a browser without support sees a present boolean attribute and hides the panel.
  • Motion durations are 120 / 180 / 280ms, not 140 / 200 / 240. The token build requires density values to reference primitives, and the existing ref.duration scale already had three steps that mean the right things. Inventing three more primitives to hit invented numbers would have been the wrong trade.
  • Two of the four proposed blocks were not built. TreatmentPlan is a nested ChartAccordion and RestrictedSection is an item state — shipping them as components would have been wrappers that add nothing. IntakeChecklist is cut for now and noted in §12.

01

The claim, and the three things that follow from it

CONTENT.md already states the governing idea for this library: an interface that omits a fact it already has is making a claim. An accordion is a machine for omitting facts on purpose. So it is the one component where that rule has to be built into the mechanism rather than into the copy.

CLAIM 01

Collapsed is not absent

If collapsing a section could change what the reader does next, the header must carry the fact that would change it. A row that says only "Assessments" costs a clinician an interaction to learn whether anything happened. Multiply by fourteen sections and you have designed a chart nobody reads.

CLAIM 02

Disclosure is an event, not a state change

Opening a substance-use section under 42 CFR Part 2, or a note held under a Cures Act exception, is an access. It may need a consent on file, a reason code, or a log entry. A component whose only output is onChange(keys) cannot express that, so every product rebuilds it badly.

CLAIM 03

The reader may be the person the record is about

Under the information-blocking rules the same section serves the clinician and the patient. Trauma-informed practice says: never ambush. A disclosure widget is exactly the right shape for you choose when this appears — but only if it knows the difference between a section that is closed and a section that is guarded.

Boundary

This component displays what the record holds. It does not compute severity, score an instrument, or interpret a result. Severity arrives as a prop from the application, alongside the text that states it. ARCHITECTURE.md §9 is the constraint: nothing in this library may present itself as clinical decision support, and an accordion that decided on its own which sections were urgent would be doing exactly that.

02

What the reference gets right, and the assumption it inherits

Three references came with the brief. Read together they describe the whole spectrum from decorative to load-bearing, which is useful: the gap between them is the product.

ReferenceWhat it gets rightWhat it cannot survive
1 — The blue accordion
Rounded cards, active card tinted, chevron end-aligned		 Selection is carried by surface, not by a border alone, so the open card survives a monochrome print. Generous 56px header height. Chevron rotates rather than swapping glyphs. Every header is identical. Four rows of "Accordion Title" is the failure mode drawn to scale: the component has no way to say anything while closed. The inherited assumption

All three are built on the FAQ model: a list of questions where the reader knows from the question whether they want the answer. That model is correct for an FAQ and wrong for a chart, because in a chart the reader does not know what the question is until they have seen the answer. "Recent assessments" tells you nothing. "PHQ-9 · 21 · severe · item 9 endorsed · 3 days ago" tells you to open it, or tells you to call someone.

03

Eleven behavioral-health surfaces, and what each one demands

This is the exploration you asked for. Each row is a real surface in a behavioral health product; the right-hand column is the component requirement it produces. The requirements are the specification — §04 is just those requirements arranged into designs.

SurfaceWhat it holdsRequirement it creates
Safety plan
Stanley-Brown SPI		 Six ordered steps: warning signs → internal coping → people and settings that distract → people to ask for help → professionals and agencies → making the environment safe. Written collaboratively, read alone, often on a phone, often at 2am. A step that cannot be collapsed. Step 5 holds the crisis numbers; a person in crisis does not scroll and does not hunt for a chevron. The component needs a pinned item that renders open and disables its own trigger — which APG explicitly provides for via aria-disabled on a visible non-collapsible panel.
Assessment battery
PHQ-9, GAD-7, C-SSRS, AUDIT, ASSIST		 Scored instruments, repeated over months. The score is the point, the item responses are the detail, and one specific item — PHQ-9 item 9 — is a suicide-risk screen validated against the C-SSRS. A summary slot with severity and trend. Progress notes
SOAP · DAP · BIRP		 One long-form note per encounter, dozens to hundreds per patient, strictly chronological. Find-in-page must work, and printing must not print an empty page. Both are broken by default in every accordion that hides with display:none. Also: lazy mount, and a real answer for 200 items.
Treatment plan Problem → goal → objective → intervention. Genuinely three levels deep, and the levels mean different things. Heading level as a prop. Substance-use record
42 CFR Part 2		 SUD treatment records carried under a separate federal confidentiality regime from the rest of the chart. The 2024 final rule aligned much of it with HIPAA and permits a single TPO consent — enforcement began 16 February 2026 — but it also created a distinct category for an SUD clinician's session notes that a broad consent does not cover. A section that announces its own existence without revealing its content, and reports when it is opened. The rule neither requires nor forbids segmentation, which means the interface decision is genuinely the product's — so the component has to be able to express it either way.
Psychotherapy notes

A chart that silently drops the section claims it does not exist.
Medication

Same summary-slot requirement, different vocabulary — and the severity chip has to route through the existing --ox-badge-* tokens so critical reads identically here and on a lab result.
Intake & onboarding Consents, ROI, insurance, demographics, screeners, first-appointment scheduling. Reference image 2 is exactly this shape. A checklist variant: per-step status, single-open policy, auto-advance on completion, and a progress statement that is text and not only a bar.
Group therapy note One session, attendance, then a per-member note that is that member's record and nobody else's. Per-item access, not per-accordion access. The gate has to sit on the item, because in one list some members' notes are open to this reader and others are not.

Progressive disclosure at the reader's pace is the core trauma-informed move; the component should make the calm version the easy one to build.
The pattern across all eleven

Five of the eleven need something no accordion library ships: a per-item notion of whether this reader may open this section, and what happens when they do. Four need the header to carry a clinical fact. Two need the component to be honest about content it will not show. None of that is exotic — it is just absent, because accordions were designed for FAQs and never revisited.

04

Seven design moves

Each one is a requirement from §03 turned into a mechanism. Six of the seven are things the component enforces rather than documents, because a rule that lives only in a guidance page is a rule that holds for about three sprints.

1 · The header is a summary, not a label

Every item gets a summary slot rendered end-aligned in the trigger, and an optional severity that paints a 3px rail down the leading edge of the header. The rule the component is built around:

If collapsing the section could change what the reader does next, the header carries the fact that would change it. A lint rule enforces the mechanical half: an item with severity set and no summary is an error, because colouring a rail red while saying nothing is exactly the "colour alone" failure CONTENT.md §3 already forbids.

2 · A section that cannot be closed

pinned renders the item open, disables its trigger, and sets aria-disabled="true" — which is precisely the case the ARIA Authoring Practices Guide carves out: set it "only if the panel is visible and the accordion prevents collapsing that panel." Crisis contacts in a safety plan are the motivating case. The trigger stays in the tab order and stays focusable, so a keyboard user still lands on it and hears the label; it simply does not toggle.

3 · The disclosure gate — the piece nobody ships

An item's access describes what standing between the reader and the content. Five values, and they are deliberately not a boolean:

ValueMeansWhat the reader sees closedWhat opening does
"open"Ordinary contentLabel and summaryOpens. Default.
"advisory"Content that may be distressing to this readerLabel, and a stated content noteShows the note and a confirm control. No log, no consent — a choice. This is the trauma-informed case, and the point is that the reader decides, not that the content is hidden.
"reason"Permitted, but the access is recordedLabel, summary, and that opening is loggedCollects a reason code, calls onDisclose, opens on resolve. Break-the-glass.
"consent"Requires a consent that is on fileLabel, the policy that governs it, and the consent's stateOpens if consent resolves; otherwise states what is missing and offers the request path. The 42 CFR Part 2 case.
"withheld"This reader cannot obtain itLabel, "Restricted — not shown", and the reasonNothing. The trigger does not toggle. The section still exists, because deleting it would claim the chart is complete.

onDisclose returns boolean | Promise<boolean>. That single signature covers a synchronous policy check, an async consent lookup, and a modal that resolves when the user confirms — and it lets the application refuse without the component having to know why. The component never decides access; it only refuses to open without an answer.

Deliberate non-goal

The component emits the event. It does not write an audit record, does not talk to a consent service, and does not persist anything. ARCHITECTURE.md §14 rules out data fetching, and an audit trail that lives in a UI component is an audit trail that disappears the first time someone renders it in a test.

4 · Find-in-page, deep links, and print

A clinician presses Ctrl+F and types clozapine. In every accordion built on display:none — which is all of them, including antd's — the browser finds nothing, and the clinician concludes the chart does not mention it. That is not a usability complaint. That is a wrong answer to a medication question.

The fix is hidden="until-found", which became Baseline in December 2025. The browser fires beforematch on the panel, we open the item, and the browser scrolls to the match. The same mechanism handles fragment navigation, so #section-medications in a URL opens the section rather than scrolling to a closed row.

↻ Revised — React cannot express this attribute

Found during implementation, and it is the reason no React accordion ships this today: React 19 treats hidden as a boolean attribute and serialises hidden="until-found" as hidden="". Verified against react-dom 19.2 in renderToString and on the client.

So the component renders the boolean and upgrades the attribute after commit. That ordering is not a workaround, it is what makes the server output correct: a panel that only became hidden after hydration would flash an entire expanded record on first paint. Between commit and effect the panel carries hidden="" — still hidden, still out of the accessibility tree, just not yet findable. React does not re-touch an attribute whose prop did not change, so the upgraded value survives every re-render in which the panel stays closed. No feature detection is involved, and the brief was wrong to ask for it: the attribute degrades on its own, because a browser without support sees a present boolean.

There is a second reason to prefer it, and it is the one that makes the animation work: hidden="until-found" is specified as content-visibility: hidden, not display: none. The element still generates a box. So it can be transitioned, and it is still removed from the accessibility tree. One attribute buys correct hiding, findable content, and an animatable panel. 

/* The recommended CSS. Author `display:grid` beats the UA's
   [hidden]{display:none}, so re-assert the hiding explicitly. */
.ox-accordion__panel {
  display: grid;
  grid-template-rows: 0fr;                  /* works everywhere */
  transition: grid-template-rows 200ms var(--ox-ease);
}
.ox-accordion__panel[hidden] { content-visibility: hidden; }
.ox-accordion__panel > .body   { overflow: hidden; min-height: 0; }
[data-open="true"] > .ox-accordion__panel { grid-template-rows: 1fr; }

/* Progressive enhancement, Chromium today (~71%): the same
   transition against a real intrinsic height. */
@supports (interpolate-size: allow-keywords) { :root { interpolate-size: allow-keywords; } }

Ordering matters and is the part implementations get wrong: on open, remove hidden before starting the transition or you animate an empty box; on close, set it on transitionend or the content vanishes before it has moved. Under prefers-reduced-motion both happen immediately.

Printing gets the same treatment from the other direction. printExpanded (default true) opens every permitted section in @media print — never a withheld one — so a printed chart is a chart and not a table of contents.

5 · Heading level is a prop

APG requires the trigger to be wrapped in an element with role heading and an aria-level "appropriate for the information architecture". The appropriate level is not knowable by the component, so headingLevel is required at the nesting boundary and defaults to 3. A nested accordion without one is a lint warning. This is the single highest-value accessibility decision in the component: for a screen-reader user, the heading list is the chart's table of contents.

6 · Region role, but only up to six

APG puts role="region" on the panel with aria-labelledby pointing at its trigger — and then says to avoid it when more than roughly six panels can be expanded at once, because a screen reader's landmark list stops being navigation and becomes noise. A fourteen-section chart in multi-open mode is exactly that case. So the component counts: region when the number of simultaneously-openable items is ≤ 6, omitted above it, and overridable with panelRole. Almost nobody implements the second half of that rule.

7 · Motion borrowed from the loaders

The five loaders in this library already argue that tempo is content — a spinner's cadence says the system is straining, a breath's says you can wait. The accordion inherits it through density rather than through a prop: 120ms clinical, 180ms standard, 280ms patient. A dense worklist should not feel slow; a patient reading their own care plan should not feel hurried. Reduced motion collapses all three to instant, and no state is carried by motion alone.

↻ Revised — the numbers came from the token build, not from taste

The brief proposed 140 / 200 / 240ms. The token build requires a density value to reference a primitive, and ref.duration already had three steps meaning fast, base and slow — 120, 180 and 280. Adding three new primitives to hit three invented numbers would have widened the palette to preserve a preference. The existing scale says the same thing, so density now carries duration as a fourth key, and every profile must define it or the build fails — the same rule that already stops a theme silently missing a status colour.

6role="tab" is set with aria-expanded and no aria-selected.Invalid ARIA. aria-selected is a required state for tab; without it the tablist has no selected tab.ARIA 1.2
7Every tab gets tabIndex={0}, and no arrow-key handling exists.A tablist is supposed to be one tab stop with arrow keys inside it. Here fourteen sections produce fourteen tab stops and no arrow navigation — precisely inverted.APG Tabs
8role="tabpanel" with no aria-labelledby.An unlabeled panel. The one thing the tab pattern buys you is the panel/tab association, and it is not wired.APG Tabs
9Hidden content is removed with display:none (ant-collapse-content-hidden).Find-in-page cannot see it and printing prints nothing. Not a WCAG failure — a clinical one. See §04 move 4.Clinical
Be fair about this

None of the above is carelessness about Collapse specifically. Ant Design ships no accessibility documentation and its maintainers' stated position on WCAG is that there are no plans yet; issue #6017, "Collapse supports ARIA", has been open for years pointing at the same APG page cited here. This is a known, declared gap, not a regression. It just happens to land on the component where the gap is structural.

The wrap-or-build decision, and why it differs from Signature's

Signature wraps antd's Modal and Tabs, and that was right: antd's focus trap and dialog lifecycle are genuinely good, and reimplementing them for a component whose whole argument is accessibility would have been the wrong trade. The gaps there were at the edges — a missing accessible name, focus landing on a sentinel — and edges are patchable from outside.

Here the defects are the DOM itself. Findings 1–8 are all "the markup antd emits is the wrong markup". You cannot add a heading wrapper, change a div to a button, or delete role="tab" from outside the component — classNames and styles, v6's new customisation surface, reach CSS and not semantics. Wrapping Collapse would mean shipping every one of those nine findings under an Oxygen label, in the library whose differentiator is that it does not do that. Recommendation: build on our own headless primitive, and match antd's API exactly.

That recommendation has a second, larger payoff, and it happens to resolve the question left open when Signature took its antd dependency. A from-scratch accordion has no antd runtime dependency, which means it still ships through the shadcn registry as copy-source. Wrapping would have made this the second component to close that channel. Building keeps both channels open and keeps the "wrap, match, or migrate" decision genuinely open rather than settled by accumulation.

type AccessDescriptor =
  | { kind: "open" }
  | { kind: "advisory";  notice: string }
  | { kind: "reason";    reasons: ReasonCode[] }
  | { kind: "consent";   policy: string; state: "granted" | "missing" | "expired"; expiresAt?: string }
  | { kind: "withheld";  reason: string };

interface DisclosureEvent {
  key: React.Key;
  access: AccessDescriptor;
  reasonCode?: string;            // present for kind: "reason"
  at: string;                     // ISO 8601 with offset — never a bare local time
}
One deliberate deviation from antd, and it is a bug fix

accordion keeps its antd name and its antd meaning — one panel open at a time. It does not change the ARIA pattern. There is no tablist, no tab, no tabpanel: the markup is identical in both modes and only the state policy differs. Finding 5 in §06 is the reason, and it is worth naming as a principle rather than a patch — a prop that changes the state policy must not change the accessibility contract. Anyone migrating from antd gets a component that behaves the same and announces itself correctly, with no code change.

This is ARCHITECTURE.md §2's argument made concrete: when a customer needs their own visual language they rebuild L2 and keep L1, and L1 is where the accessibility correctness lives — the part they should not be rewriting. The hook has no DOM opinion, no styling, and no antd.

Eight axes of customisation

"Super customisable" is easy to claim and usually means a large props object. The useful test is different: how far can a customer diverge before they have to fork? Because in this library a fork is permanent — a copied file has no upgrade channel — the override points are the thing that keeps customisation and maintenance compatible.

Architecture, and the data structure that removes the special cases

The nine findings in §06 share one cause, and it is not sloppiness. It is that rc-collapse models the accordion as a rendering problem with a boolean modifier, so accordion became an if in the render path and dragged a whole second ARIA pattern in behind it. Get the state model right and that branch has nowhere to live.

The state is a set and a policy, not a mode

type AccordionState = {
  open: ReadonlySet<Key>;
  pending: ReadonlyMap<Key, DisclosureRequest>;   // gates in flight
};

// Every mode the component supports is this function, and nothing else.
type Policy = (open: ReadonlySet<Key>, key: Key, items: Item[]) => Set<Key>;

const POLICIES: Record<PolicyName, Policy> = {
  multiple:   (open, k) => toggle(open, k),
  single:     (open, k) => open.has(k) ? empty() : only(k),
  exclusive:  (open, k) => only(k),                       // cannot close by clicking
  atLeastOne: (open, k) => open.has(k) && open.size === 1 ? open : toggle(open, k),
};

// Pinned is not a branch either — it is a filter applied after the policy,
// so a pinned item is simply one the policy is not permitted to remove.
const next = union(POLICIES[policy](state.open, key, items), pinnedKeys(items));

Four modes, one pinning rule, one gate mechanism — and zero conditionals in the render path. The markup is identical for every configuration, which is exactly what makes the accessibility contract impossible to vary by accident. The bug in §06 finding 5 is not reachable from this shape.

Performance, and the rule about measurement

  • No measurement in the toggle path. No ResizeObserver, no scrollHeight read, no forced reflow to compute a target height. The 0fr → 1fr grid transition is pure CSS. Every accordion that animates by measuring gets slower as sections get longer, and a 142-encounter note list is where that shows up.
  • Lazy mount, retained after. mount: "lazy" | "eager" | "unmount". Lazy is the default; unmount maps to antd's destroyOnHidden.
  • content-visibility: auto plus contain-intrinsic-size on closed panels, so a hundred collapsed sections cost layout once rather than every frame.
  • SSR and RSC safe. Open state derives from props on the server; hidden="until-found" is a server-renderable attribute, so there is no post-hydration flash and no layout effect in the critical path.
  • Virtualisation is an escape hatch, not the default. Above roughly 200 items, renderItem plus a windowing adapter. Below it, do not — a virtualised list breaks find-in-page, which is the thing §04 move 4 just fixed.

What only you can decide

These were open when the brief was written. All five were decided during implementation — the call taken is stated first, then the reasoning that was already here.

Decided
  • 1 · Build, not wrap. No antd dependency, so the registry channel survives.
  • 2 · All five access kinds in v1, not the reduced pair. The gate is the differentiator and the async path is what makes it real; halving it would have shipped the easy half.
  • 3 · Reviewed default copy, replaceable through locale. Every string the behaviour layer needs is in one object with English defaults.
  • 4 · Accordion / Disclosure / ChartAccordion / SafetyPlan. ChartAccordion over ChartSections — discoverability wins for a name people will search for.
  • 5 · SafetyPlan is the demo. It is the most specific to behavioral health and the pinned crisis step is the clearest single-frame argument in this document.

1 · Wrap antd's Collapse, or build on our own primitive

Recommendation: build. §06 is the argument — the defects are the DOM, and classNames/styles reach CSS but not semantics, so wrapping means shipping nine findings under an Oxygen label. Building also keeps the registry channel open, which matters more than this one component: Signature closed it for itself, and a second antd-dependent component would start closing it by accumulation rather than by decision. The counter-argument is real and worth stating: building means we own an accordion's keyboard and focus behaviour forever, and antd will keep shipping fixes we no longer inherit. For this component that trade is clearly right; it is not obviously right for the next one.

2 · Is the disclosure gate in v1, or does the app own it?

It is the differentiator, and it is also the part most likely to collide with how a customer already does consent. A middle option worth considering: ship withheld and advisory in v1 — both are purely presentational and need no service — and hold reason and consent for v1.1 once a real customer's audit contract is in front of us. That halves phase 3 and loses none of the design argument.

3 · How opinionated should the patient-facing gate be?

A content warning is a clinical and ethical position, not only a UI one — badly worded it becomes a deterrent rather than a choice. The component can supply the mechanism and leave every string to the customer, or it can ship default copy that has been through clinical review. The second is worth more and costs a reviewer. Given the revenue model is services and contracts rather than volume, the reviewed default is probably the asset.

4 · Naming

The loaders are named poetically because they are decorative; structural components in this library take plain names. So: Accordion for the many, Disclosure for the one — which also matches React Aria's split and the ARIA vocabulary. Clinical compositions take the domain's own words: ChartAccordion, RestrictedSection, SafetyPlan, AssessmentPanel, IntakeChecklist. The one open question is whether the composed clinical component should be ChartAccordion or ChartSections; the first is more discoverable, the second describes what it is rather than how it moves.

5 · Which surface goes first

If the point is a demo that sells, SafetyPlan is the one — it is the most specific to behavioral health, the pinned crisis step is the clearest single-frame argument in this brief, and nobody else's component library can render it. If the point is coverage, ChartAccordion is the one, because ten of the eleven surfaces in §03 are instances of it.
