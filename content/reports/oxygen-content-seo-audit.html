Content · SEO · Generative discovery

The writing is good.
The architecture around it is not.

A page-by-page audit of oxygenui.design, measured against the running site on 28 August 2026. The prose is specific, technically literate and free of marketing filler — measurably so. What fails is everything structural: how it is organised, how much of it arrives at once, and whether a search engine or an answer engine can find any of it.

01 — Executive summary

The problem is not the sentences.

Start with what is working, because it is unusual. I scanned roughly 8,000 words of prose for the vocabulary that marks generated marketing copy — powerful, seamless, robust, cutting-edge, next-generation, world-class, unlock, empower, leverage, elevate. Zero occurrences. Instead the site cites WCAG criteria by number, names FHIR resources per component, and makes competitive claims by measurement: @rc-component/table ships one aria-* attribute in its entire build. That is a rare and genuinely defensible voice. Almost nothing in this report asks you to change it.

What fails is the architecture around the writing. /components renders 27 components beneath a single <h2>, so the catalogue has no outline for a reader skimming or a crawler indexing. Every static page ships a meta description over the display limit, so every one truncates in results. The home page has no metadata export at all. Ten statically-generated pages are absent from the sitemap. Four of five page H1s use the identical grammatical construction, and not one of them contains a term anybody searches for.

And there is too much of it arriving at once. 125 prose sentences exceed 35 words. The date picker page alone carries 32, stacked six-deep beneath its demos — which is precisely the “messy and confusing” already reported. The fix is almost never deletion. This reasoning is the most valuable content on the site; it simply should not all be the first thing between a reader and a component.

Protect

The measured claims. The absence of adjectives. The clinical reasoning behind each component — it is the moat, and no competitor has written it. Defer it; never cut it.

Fix first

Meta descriptions, home-page metadata, canonicals, sitemap coverage, and an outline for the catalogue. Seven P0 items, roughly two engineering days, and they are the ones blocking discovery entirely.

Composite: 6.3 / 10. The distribution matters more than the mean. Technical depth at 9 and brand voice at 8 sit beside information hierarchy at 4 and SEO at 4 — this is a site whose content is better than its ability to deliver that content to anyone. Every low score is structural, and structure is the cheap thing to fix.

05 — Content to add

Six gaps, ordered by who is blocked.

The pattern across all six: the site is written for a developer already evaluating components, and for nobody who has to approve, integrate or maintain them.

P0A build-vs-buy page for engineering leaders

Nothing on the site addresses the decision an engineering director actually makes. One page: what it costs to build these 27 components in-house, what breaks when you do, what Oxygen does not cover.

P0Bundle size and runtime cost, per component

The single most-asked question about any component library, and the site does not answer it once. The hero claims Runtime: 0 without a figure behind it.

P1Framework and design-system compatibility

Next.js App Router, RSC boundaries, Vite, Remix. Tailwind version. antd v6 interop — ADR 0010 settles this internally and the site never says so.

P1Migration and troubleshooting notes

The four extraction traps found while documenting props are exactly the content developers search for. They exist in test comments and nowhere a reader can reach.

P1A comparison page

“Oxygen UI vs building it yourself” and “vs antd Table for clinical grids”. The measured @rc-component/table finding is the strongest asset here and appears in one paragraph.

P2Versioning and support policy

Enterprise buyers screen on this before they screen on features. Semver policy, breaking-change cadence, LTS intent.

What is already right

The sitemap is derived from the catalogue rather than hand-listed, with a comment explaining that a hand-written list is the one nobody remembers to update. Priority is set by maturity so an experimental component cannot outrank a stable one. Component descriptions are all inside the limit. This is careful work — it just stops at components.

The structural gap

Ten statically-generated pages — six showcase blocks and four marketplace packs — appear in no sitemap, have no breadcrumbs, and are reachable only by clicking through an index. They are the most link-worthy pages on the domain and they are functionally unlisted. The fix is to spread BLOCKS and the shelf into sitemap.ts exactly as CATALOG already is.

Keyword strategy, stated plainly. Not one H1 on this site contains a term a buyer would type. That is a defensible choice for the home page, where the line earns attention — but it should not be a site-wide pattern. Realistic targets, none requiring a word of filler: FHIR React components, healthcare UI component library, EHR interface components, accessible clinical data grid, WCAG 2.2 healthcare components. Each maps to a page that already exists and already earns the ranking on substance.

07 — AI & generative discovery

The strongest asset, and the thinnest.

llms.txt — keep doing this

163 lines. Every component with its description, install line, full state list and maturity. A disclaimer block establishing what the library is not. Then, unusually, explicit code-generation rules: a missing value renders as explicitly missing; an uninterpreted result reads “Not interpreted”, never “Normal”; never build a Tailwind class from a variable. That last one is a real failure mode being pre-empted in a machine-readable file. Very few libraries have thought this far.

Structured data — one type, one route

The domain emits SoftwareSourceCode on component pages and nothing anywhere else. There is no Organization, no WebSite, no BreadcrumbList, no FAQPage. Answer engines resolve entities before they quote them, and there is currently no machine-readable statement of what Oxygen UI is as an entity.

The highest-leverage GEO change is free. Every component page already has an accessibility section written as answers to implicit questions — which keyboard keys move focus, what a screen reader announces, how the state survives forced-colors. Marking those up as FAQPage costs nothing in authoring and turns already-written content into the exact structure answer engines quote verbatim.

Second: the one-line technical description should be written once and reused three times — as the meta description, the llms.txt entry, and the page lede. Today those are three separately-authored strings per component, which means three chances to drift and no single canonical sentence for a model to learn.

08 — Component page standard

Eleven sections, in this order, every time.

Close to the direction you set. Two changes: “Why it exists” moves above usage, because it is the strongest content the library has and it is currently buried; and performance becomes conditional rather than mandatory, because an empty section reads worse than no section.

#SectionWhenStandard
1Name + one-line technical descriptionAlwaysWhat it is and what it is typed to. One sentence, under 15 words. This is also the meta description and the llms.txt entry — write it once, use it three times.
2Live demo, states switchableAlwaysAbove the fold. The demo is the argument; everything below explains it.
3InstallAlwaysOne line. Setup notes are page-level, not per-component.
4Why it existsAlwaysThe clinical problem, in 2–3 sentences. Currently the strongest content on the site and currently buried.
5UsageAlwaysThe best default implementation, copyable, no ellipses.
6States & variantsAlwaysEvery state with the props that produce it. Already built as the state browser.
7API referenceAlwaysStacked list, not a table — a 300-character union does not fit a column.
8AccessibilityAlwaysKeyboard map, ARIA pattern, contrast, screen-reader behaviour. Write as Q&A; it is what answer engines quote.
9PerformanceWhere non-trivialBundle size, render cost, virtualisation notes. Currently missing everywhere.
10Healthcare usageWhere applicableThe realistic scenario, named. Behavioural-health specifics where they differ.
11Related componentsAlways3–5 links. Internal linking is currently the weakest structural signal on the site.

09 — Voice & writing standard

Codify what is already happening.

The voice on this site is good and undocumented, which means it survives only as long as the people holding it in their heads. These are the rules it already follows.

Working

“An uninterpreted result reads ‘Not interpreted’, never ‘Normal’.”A rule, a consequence, and a concrete string. Nothing to interpret.
“Status is never colour alone.”Five words. States a policy that can be tested.
“@rc-component/table emits one aria-* attribute in its entire build.”A measured number beats every adjective available.

Not working

“A disclosure widget whose headers can be read while closed, with a per-section access model for content a reader may not simply be shown.”Two ideas welded with a comma. Split it, or move half to the detail page.
Four of five page H1s: “[noun] that [surprising verb].”Distinctive once. A formula by the fifth page. Vary the construction.
“Components built for the moments a healthcare interface is judged: …”Thirty-four words before the reader learns what is being sold.
RuleStandard
Sentence lengthTarget 15–18 words. Hard ceiling 30. One idea per sentence; a comma joining two independent clauses is usually two sentences.
Numbers over adjectives“27 components, 555 documented props, WCAG 2.2 AA in CI” — never “comprehensive” or “rigorous”.
Bannedpowerful · seamless · robust · cutting-edge · beautiful · next-generation · revolutionary · world-class · unlock · empower · leverage · elevate. Currently at zero. Keep it there.
Terminology“component”, not “widget”. “source”, not “code snippet”. “state”, not “variation”. “install”, not “get started”. Pick one and never alternate for variety.
CapitalisationSentence case for all headings. Component names in Title Case. Props and packages in code voice, always.
CTAsName the outcome: “Install Result Value”, “Read the props”. Never “Learn more”, “Get started”, “Explore”.
The claim testEvery sentence must survive: could a competitor write this about their product? If yes, it is not saying anything.
