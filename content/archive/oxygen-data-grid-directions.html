Data Grid · five directions

Five arguments about what a worklist is.

Same fixture in every one — 24 rows of 1,438, a filter somebody set this morning, one model-derived column, three results that have landed and not been let in. What changes is the argument the component makes, and each argument produces a different component, a different API and a different home-page demo. Pick one and I build it: the registry component, its tests and stories, the docs page, and the animated section that replaces the current preview.

Coverage is not optional

24 on screen, 1,438 in the cohort, and the predicate written as a sentence you can print. Every direction below states it; they disagree about where.

Sorting is a clinical act

A sort by a model-derived column names the model, its version and the population it was validated on. No other grid does this.

Nothing moves under the hand

Results arrive and wait. A grid that reorders under a pointer is how somebody actions the row that used to be there.

Absence is a word, never a dash

Awaiting, restricted, not recorded and refused are four different facts. An em dash claims they are one.

Direction 01

The Ledger

The grid is a document, and it prints correctly. Hairline rules, tabular figures, a header block like the top of a lab report, and provenance as a numbered footnote — because that is how a real clinical report cites a derived value. No cards, no shadows, no motion required for it to make its point.

Wins
Density. Legibility at 12px. Prints and photocopies. Cheapest to build and to keep correct.
Costs
Quietest on a landing page. The animated demo has to be typographic, not kinetic.
Feels like
Bloomberg terminal meets a signed lab report.

Direction 02

The Instrument

The site already has an instrument language — dark bezel, trace green, monospace readouts. This takes it the whole way: a signal rail down the left showing arrivals as they land, and a status line along the bottom that reads out the focused row by name and MRN. Row identity is re-stated at the point of action, which is the one control that measurably reduces wrong-patient errors.

Wins
Unmistakably ours. The status line is the safety argument made visible. Best home-page presence.
Costs
Dark-first; needs a light answer that keeps the character. The rail is decoration unless it is fed real signal.
Feels like
A ward monitor with a command line.

Direction 03

Strata

A clinical grid is many small charts side by side, not text in boxes. Potassium is drawn as a mark on its own reference range, so 6.8 is visibly outside and 4.1 is visibly inside without anybody reading a number. Risk is a bar. Next-due is an arc that empties. Every visual carries its number as text beside it, so nothing is carried by position alone.

Wins
Comparison across rows is instant. The reference range is the fact everyone re-derives in their head.
Costs
Rows are twice as tall — 12 on screen, not 24. Most expensive to build and to keep accessible.
Feels like
A flowsheet that finally admits it is a chart.

Direction 04

The Triage Board

A worklist is a queue of obligations, not a list of people — so the rows are things to do, and one patient can legitimately appear twice. Rows live in consequence bands that they never cross under your hand; arrivals dock at the top of their own band. Sorting happens inside a band, so a model cannot promote a patient out of "watch" into "act now".

Wins
Matches how a charge nurse actually reads a board. Makes the "one person, three rows" idea visible.
Costs
A strong opinion baked into the component. Not every grid is a queue; the plain table has to still exist.
Feels like
A handover board that knows what is owed.
Obligations · 4-West · shift 07:00–19:00

Bands are policy, not a model. The derived column ranks rows within a band and never moves one across — a prediction may order work, it may not decide that work is urgent.

Direction 05

The Frozen Stream

Make the live-data argument the whole design. A gutter on the right is a time axis of what has arrived; held results stack in it as ticks. Put a pointer in the body and a hairline band says FROZEN — the grid has stopped accepting changes while your hand is over it, and it says so rather than doing it silently. One button releases the queue and the new rows land with a highlight.

Wins
The one thing no competitor ships, made the hero. Demos itself; the animation is the argument.
Costs
A static screenshot says little. The gutter costs 8.5rem of width that a dense worklist would rather spend on a column.
Feels like
A monitor that shows you what it is deliberately not telling you yet.
