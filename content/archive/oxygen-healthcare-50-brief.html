01 · Executive summary

The market for a healthcare component library is not designers. It is the four engineers who keep rebuilding the same chart header.

Every company building an EHR, a behavioral-health platform, a care-management product or a clinical AI tool builds the same fifty components. Not similar components — the same ones. A patient context header. A result value. A medication row. An assessment runner. A grid of patients with a risk column. A dockable AI assistant. They build them badly, twice, because the first attempt did not model absence and the second did not model permission.

The reason they keep building them is not that the components are hard to draw. It is that they are hard to get right, and the failure mode is invisible: a component that renders a preliminary potassium identically to a final one passes every test, looks correct in every screenshot, and is wrong in a way that reaches a patient.

Why specialisation is the whole argument

A generic component library competes against every other generic component library on aesthetics and bundle size, into a market of every React developer alive. A healthcare component library competes against the customer's own engineering team, and wins on a completely different axis: whether the component already encodes knowledge the team would otherwise have to acquire from a clinician.

That reframing matters commercially. It means the goal is not to be prettier than shadcn. It is to be the component where a healthcare engineering lead reads the prop table, sees dataAbsentReason handled as seven distinct states, and realises we have been further into this problem than they have.

Behavioral health is first class Eleven of the fifty exist only because behavioral health is structurally different — different instruments, different confidentiality law, different documentation obligations, different settings, different billing. This is the category nobody serious has served.

The part that is already paid for

Phase two is not greenfield. 7 of the 15 shared engines in §9 already exist in this repository. 3 ship outright — the timeline engine, the FHIR adapter layer and the clinical status system — and 4 are partly built: the patient-context provider, the AI interaction runtime, the document composition engine and the terminology service. 29 of the fifty compose over at least one engine that ships today, tested and gated. That is the difference between a plan and a wish.

The one-sentence version

We are not selling components. We are selling the two years of domain acquisition that a healthcare product team would otherwise have to do on their own patients.

02 · Research findings

Ten patterns that recur across every category of healthcare software, and what each one costs

What follows is drawn from the published literature, the current regulatory record, and the observable behaviour of the products in each category. Where a claim has a number attached, the source is in §17.

Finding 01 · Documentation burden

The clinician is a data-entry clerk, and behavioral health has it worst

Physicians spend the majority of an eleven-hour day inside the EHR. In behavioral health the ratio is worse — one to two hours of documentation for every hour of direct care — because the note must simultaneously satisfy the clinician, the payer's medical-necessity reviewer, and a state auditor.

What that implies for components: the winning documentation component is not a nicer text editor. It is one that produces an auditable note as a by-product of clinical work — which is what TreatmentPlanCanvas and SessionNoteComposer are built around.

Finding 02 · Alert fatigue

Ninety per cent of interaction alerts are overridden, and improving the alerts has not moved it

A 2024 meta-analysis puts physician override at 90% overall and 95.1% for drug–drug interaction alerts. A decade of tuning commercial alert content did not change the rate. The problem is not the content; it is the modality — an interruptive modal per pair, arriving at the moment of least receptivity.

What that implies: stop shipping interruptive alerts. Show the set at the moment of review, ranked, with the pairs the prescriber has already accepted collapsed. That is InteractionMatrix, and it is a fundamentally different component from a warning dialog.

Finding 03 · Ambient AI became standard, and its failure mode is omission

The scribe is solved. The review surface is not.

Ambient documentation is now market-standard: measured reductions of roughly 20–30% in documentation-related burnout, and double-digit minutes returned per clinician per day. But the narrative reviews are consistent that current systems still produce omissions and intermittent factual errors.

What that implies: the defensible component is not the transcription. It is AIDraftReview — the surface where a clinician accepts, rejects and amends spans, and where every accepted span keeps its provenance into the signed note.

Finding 04 · Regulation moved from future to present

Four rules that are live right now and that no component library models

  • HTI-1 DSI — certified health IT must surface 31 plain-language source attributes for every predictive decision-support intervention.
  • 42 CFR Part 2 — OCR began accepting complaints on 16 February 2026. SUD counselling notes get heightened protection when kept accessible only to the authoring clinician.
  • FDA CDS guidance, revised 6 January 2026 — the non-device test now asks software to prioritise decision-relevant details and avoid information overload, which is a user-interface requirement written into a device-classification document.
  • CMS-0057-F — four payer FHIR APIs including prior authorisation, by 1 January 2027.

What that implies: four components with almost no competition — ModelTransparencyCard, ConsentDisclosureManager, EvidenceCard and PriorAuthTracker.

Finding 05 · The golden thread is unbuilt

A note that never names the goal it advanced is, to an auditor, documentation of a conversation

Behavioral-health reimbursement depends on a demonstrable chain: assessment → problem → goal → objective → intervention → progress note → outcome. Practices increasingly run an EHR and a separate AI scribe, and the scribe has never seen the treatment plan — so the note reads beautifully and proves nothing.

What that implies: the linkage is a data contract between two components, not a feature of either. It is why TreatmentPlanCanvas exports objective handles and SessionNoteComposer refuses to sign without consuming one.

Finding 06 · Absence is unmodelled everywhere

The em dash is the most dangerous character in healthcare software

Across every product category reviewed, missing data renders as a blank, a dash, or a zero. A patient who declined a draw, a test that was cancelled, a haemolysed specimen, a value the interface dropped, and a result you lack permission to see are five different clinical facts collapsed into one glyph.

What that implies: absence modelling is the single cheapest differentiator in the library, and it touches roughly thirty of the fifty components.

Finding 07 · Everything is a grid, and every grid is bespoke

Three structurally different grids keep being built with one wrong abstraction

Healthcare needs a record grid (patients, claims, orders), a flowsheet (time as columns, measures as rows — vitals, I&O, the MAR, q15 observations) and a registry (a panel with computed columns and a review cadence). Teams reach for a generic data grid, discover the flowsheet is transposed and virtualised on the wrong axis, and hand-roll it.

What that implies: one grid engine, three components — ClinicalDataGrid, FlowsheetGrid, CaseloadRegistry.

Finding 08 · Context errors are a design problem, not a training problem

Wrong-patient documentation survives every amount of staff education

Multi-chart workflows, interruption-driven work and identically named patients produce a steady rate of wrong-patient orders and misfiled notes. The Joint Commission's two-identifier rule addresses the bedside; nothing addresses the eleven browser tabs.

What that implies: patient context must be a guarded runtime value rather than a variable — which is why PatientGuard already ships, and why RecentPatientStack tints per chart and re-asserts identity on return.

Finding 09 · Measurement-based care is now contractual, and its UI is a line chart

Payers increasingly require repeated instruments; nobody renders the thresholds that make them mean anything

A PHQ-9 moving from 14 to 12 is noise: the reliable-change threshold is 5 points, response is a 50% reduction, remission is below 5. Products plot the raw series and leave the clinician to hold three thresholds in their head.

What that implies: ScoreTrajectory draws the thresholds, not just the points — and refuses to plot instruments against each other when their scales are not commensurable.

Finding 10 · Behavioral health is a different product, not a theme

Five structural differences that break generic clinical components

  • Instruments, not observations. Scored, branching, licensed, item-level-risky.
  • Different law. Part 2 segmentation; psychotherapy notes stored separately by statute; minor consent that varies by state and by service.
  • Different documentation. The golden thread; medical necessity restated every session.
  • Different settings. Group, IOP/PHP, residential, crisis, and a telehealth majority.
  • Different billing. Time-based psychotherapy codes; CoCM minute-accumulation across a month with a psychiatric consultant's registry review as a condition of payment.

What that implies: eleven components that a general-purpose healthcare library will never build, and the reason a behavioral-health platform would choose us over a horizontal competitor.

04 · Design system

Stock Ant Design defaults were the generic look. This is what replaced them.

Every number in the before/after table was measured across the fifty mockups by script, not estimated.

The first version of this catalogue rendered every mockup in Ant Design v6's default token values. That was a deliberate choice and it was the wrong one. antd's defaults — #1677ff, a uniform 6 px radius, a white card with a hairline and a three-layer drop shadow — are the most widely deployed enterprise look on the web. A healthcare library that ships them is visually indistinguishable from every other antd application, which is a positioning failure before it is an aesthetic one.

05 · Interaction and motion

The catalogue's weakest axis was that nothing moved. This section is live — hover it.

The audit in §10 found that the catalogue's weakest axis by a wide margin was interaction — 7.1 out of 10, against 9.9 for workflow modelling. The components were still frames. Static mockups can describe a hover; they cannot be judged on one.

What the motion layer refuses to do

No entrance animation on data

A lab result does not fade in. Content that arrives after a wait appears; only content the user caused to appear is animated.

No animated progress without progress

An indeterminate wait gets a paced loader from the shipping set. A moving bar that does not track anything is a lie, and it is the lie clinicians notice.

No motion carrying meaning alone

The critical pulse is redundant with a hue, a triangle and the word unacknowledged. Under prefers-reduced-motion every duration collapses to 1 ms and nothing is lost.

01

ClinicalStatus<Status scale kind />

One status vocabulary for the whole library, in which a state that cannot be rendered without colour cannot be rendered at all.

CoreMicro
The problem

Every healthcare product invents its own status colours, six times, in six teams. The results disagree: amber means pending in the lab module and abnormal in the vitals module, and a clinician who learns one is actively misled by the other. Worse, the encoding is almost always colour plus a word in a colour-matched hue — which collapses in forced-colors mode, in monochrome print, and for the 8% of male clinicians with a red–green deficiency.

The fix is not a nicer palette. It is a closed vocabulary: a fixed set of scales, each with fixed steps, each step carrying a hue, a shape and a word that are chosen together and cannot be used apart.

Primary users

Every clinician, every screen. Also the design system team, who stop arbitrating colour arguments.

Where it is used

Result criticality, encounter state, order status, data quality, access class, task urgency, AI verification state.

Behavioral-health relevance

Adds the scales generic libraries omit: risk level (from a screener), engagement state (engaged / disengaged / lost to contact), level-of-care, and consent class under 42 CFR Part 2.

The interface

A chip, a dot, or a table-cell affix — three presentations of one datum. The shape glyph is not decoration and not an icon: it is a second channel carrying the same bit. Critical is a filled triangle; unknown is a hatched square; restricted is a lock outline; pending is a hollow ring. A reader who cannot distinguish the hues reads the shape; a reader with a screen reader reads the word.

Functionality
  • Nine scales, fixed steps, no free-text status
  • Hue + shape + word, emitted together or not at all
  • Density-aware: 22px compact, 26px default
  • aria-label composed from the scale name and the step, not just the step
  • Table-affix mode: a 3px row rule instead of a chip, for grids at 40+ rows
Interaction model

Non-interactive by default. Optional onExplain turns the chip into a button that opens the definition of the step — because the meaning of preliminary is not obvious and guessing it is a clinical act.

Responsive behaviour

Identical across breakpoints. Below 360px the chip drops to glyph + abbreviated word with the full word in the accessible name — never to glyph alone.

Accessibility

Contrast floors enforced in the token build (4.5:1 text, 3:1 the glyph as a non-text indicator, per WCAG 1.4.11). forced-colors tested: chips fall back to system colours and the glyph carries the whole signal.

Technical architecture

Pure function of (scale, step, density) → class list. No state, no context, no effects. Compiles to a single span with two children; ~40 lines. The vocabulary lives in @oxygenui-design/intl so a step always has both a clinician phrasing and a patient phrasing.

FHIR

Maps Observation.status, Observation.interpretation, AllergyIntolerance.criticality, Encounter.status, Task.status, ServiceRequest.status, Consent.provision.type and DetectedIssue.severity onto the scales, one adapter each.

Dependencies

None beyond clsx. No icon library — the glyphs are CSS shapes, so the chip costs no network request.

02

ResultValue<ResultValue observation />

A single observation rendered so that the four ways it can lie to you are all impossible.

CoreMicro
The problem

The most dangerous component in healthcare software is the one that renders a number. Four failures recur, and every one of them looks perfect on screen:

  • A preliminary result rendered identically to a final one. The clinician acts; the result changes at 04:00.
  • A result with no reference range rendered as if it were normal, because nothing was highlighted.
  • A corrected result that silently replaced the value the clinician read an hour ago and documented.
  • An absent value rendered as an em dash, indistinguishable from a rendering bug, a cancelled test, a haemolysed specimen and a patient who declined the draw.
Primary users

Everyone who reads a number: physicians, nurses, pharmacists, care managers, and the patient in the portal.

Where it is used

Lab results, vitals, screening scores, device readings, point-of-care tests, patient-reported measures.

Behavioral-health relevance

Screening scores are observations too. A PHQ-9 of 14 with status: amended because the patient revised item 9 after the session is exactly this component's problem, with a much higher consequence.

The interface

Value, unit and interpretation on one line with tabular figures; the qualifiers on a second line only when they exist. The corrected case shows both values — the current one live, the superseded one struck through with the time it changed — because the clinician's memory of the old number is the actual hazard.

Functionality
  • Seven absence reasons, each rendered distinctly, none as an em dash
  • Interpretation precedence: a stated Observation.interpretation always wins over any derived one
  • Reference range shown inline with age / sex / SPCU qualification when the range carries it
  • Delta against the prior value with the interval, suppressed when the analyte or method changed
  • Corrected results render the superseded value, not just a badge
  • Provenance affix: device, lab, patient-reported, externally received, AI-extracted
Interaction model

Hover or focus opens a popover with the full specimen chain: collected, received, resulted, verified, released to patient. Click routes to the report. Keyboard reachable when interactive, inert when not — never a focusable div with no action.

Responsive behaviour

At narrow widths the qualifier line wraps below the value and the range moves into the popover; the interpretation word never truncates.

Accessibility

The accessible name is the whole clinical sentence — Potassium 6.8 millimoles per litre, critical high, reference 3.5 to 5.1, final, resulted 41 minutes ago — because a screen-reader user should not have to assemble that from five nodes.

Technical architecture

Presentational, no fetching. Takes a normalised ResultValue shape or a FHIR Observation via fromFHIR(). Memoised on (id, meta.versionId); a grid of 500 rows re-renders none of them when a filter changes.

FHIR

Observation: value[x], dataAbsentReason, status, interpretation, referenceRange (incl. appliesTo), method, device, specimen, note. Renders DataAbsentReason as seven distinct states rather than one blank.

Dependencies

None. @oxygenui-design/fhir for the adapter, tree-shaken away if unused.

03

AllergyChip<AllergyChip allergy />

The chip that refuses to conflate how bad the last reaction was with how bad the next one could be.

CoreMicro
The problem

FHIR AllergyIntolerance carries two severity-shaped fields that mean different things. criticality is the clinician's judgement of the risk of a future life-threatening reaction. reaction.severity describes how bad a past reaction was. A patient whose only documented reaction was mild urticaria can still be criticality: high — that is the whole point of the field. Primary users

The interface

Substance first, at full weight. Criticality as the primary chip. Manifestation as secondary text, never as the headline. Functionality

Responsive behaviour

Primary users

Anyone scanning a list of values; care managers on a panel; clinicians in a flowsheet.

Lab series, vitals, weights, scores, adherence, utilisation, any repeated measure.

The interface

Twelve to sixty pixels of sparkline, a direction glyph, and a delta. The valence is supplied by the caller and drives the colour; the shape carries the direction independently. Where comparability breaks, the line breaks too, with a worded reason on hover — a dashed gap is never left to be interpreted.

Functionality
  • Refuses to render a trend below minPoints (default 3) — shows the points instead
  • Breaks the series at an assay, method, unit or instrument change and says why

Hover or focus shows the point value and date. In a grid, arrow keys move point to point when the cell has focus.

Responsive behaviour

Fixed aspect; the caller sets width. Below 40px wide it degrades to the direction glyph plus delta, never to an unreadable line.

Accessibility

An accompanying visually-hidden table with the series is emitted when describedBy is not supplied — a sparkline with no text alternative is unreadable and unsearchable.

Technical architecture

One SVG path, computed in a useMemo keyed on the series identity. No chart library. 1.4 kB. Renders identically on the server.

FHIR

Consumes an Observation series; reads method, device, valueQuantity.unit and referenceRange to decide comparability.

The problem

Serious clinical work is comparative: this admission against the last one, the note against the result it is about, the plan against the session. Every EHR forces sequential navigation, and clinicians work around it with multiple browser windows — which reintroduces the wrong-patient problem and loses all state.

Generic layout libraries exist and are good. What they lack is any notion that a pane has a patient, that two panes showing different patients need to be visibly different, and that some content must never be side by side — a psychotherapy note beside a shared screen in a consultation room, for instance.

Primary users

Power users, comparative review, teaching settings, multidisciplinary meetings, tumour and treatment-team boards.

Where it is used

Any workspace where two things need to be seen at once: chart and note, two encounters, plan and session, results and orders.

Behavioral-health relevance

Treatment-team meetings review several patients in sequence with the plan, the score trajectory and the session history side by side. Screen sharing in those meetings is exactly where the psychotherapy-note boundary needs to be enforced by the layout, not by the presenter's memory.

The interface

Panes with visible patient identity and per-patient accent. Drag to split, drop to dock, pull out to float. Layouts are saveable and nameable, so the layout I use for a discharge becomes a one-click state. The presenting mode is a first-class layout that hides everything that should not be on a shared screen and says what it hid.

Functionality
  • Split, dock, float, tab, with keyboard equivalents for every drag (WCAG 2.5.7)
  • Per-pane patient identity with accent inheritance from the chart stack
  • Cross-patient layouts require an explicit acknowledgement and stay visibly distinct
  • Named, saveable layouts per user and per role
  • Presenting mode that hides restricted content and states what it hid
  • Layout persists across sessions and degrades sensibly to a smaller screen
  • Panes are lazily mounted; an unfocused pane holds state, not a live subscription
Interaction model

Every drag has a keyboard path through a move menu. Splitting is ⌘\; closing a pane with unsaved work asks. Layouts are switched from the command palette.

Responsive behaviour

Desktop: full layout engine. Tablet: at most two panes, with the split fixed at 50%. Mobile: a single pane with tab switching — the engine collapses to a stack rather than offering unusable miniature panes.

Accessibility

Panes are labelled regions with their patient in the label. Focus order follows visual order after a rearrangement. Resizing has keyboard equivalents with announced sizes.

Technical architecture

A layout tree serialised per user, with panes addressed by a stable id so a saved layout survives a release. Only visible panes are mounted; state for hidden panes is retained but their subscriptions are torn down, which is what keeps a six-pane layout from opening six live connections.

FHIR

No direct mapping. Panes carry a patient context that participates in the same guard as everything else.

ChartStateBoundary<ChartState status />

One boundary for loading, empty, stale, offline, conflicted and restricted — because in a chart those six are different clinical facts, and a spinner says none of them.

CoreComposite
The problem

System states are treated as UI chrome and in a clinical application they are clinical information. A section that is loading, one that is genuinely empty, one that is showing data from nine hours ago because the connection dropped, and one that is restricted all look approximately the same in most products: blank, or a spinner, or a grey box.

The worst of the six is stale. A vitals panel showing eleven-hour-old numbers with no indication is the most dangerous thing a component can do, because it renders perfectly and the reader has no reason to doubt it.

Skeletons deserve a specific mention: a skeleton that draws six rows implies six rows of data are coming. When the answer is zero, that is a small lie told hundreds of times a day.

Primary users

Everyone, constantly, mostly without noticing — which is the point.

Where it is used

Every data-bearing region in a clinical application.

Behavioral-health relevance

Behavioral-health charts are more likely than most to have legitimately empty sections and legitimately restricted ones, and the difference between no substance use history and the substance use history is segmented and you cannot see it is not a nuance.

The interface

One component wrapping any region, taking a status. Loading draws structure without implying quantity. Empty says which of the several kinds of empty it is. Stale keeps the data visible — hiding it is worse — with its age and a retry. Offline states what still works. Conflict shows both versions and refuses to pick. Restricted states that content exists and how to reach it, where saying so is itself permitted.

Functionality
  • Six states, each with distinct visual and textual treatment
  • Skeletons that draw structure, never a fixed number of rows
  • Staleness thresholds per data type — vitals go stale in minutes, a problem list does not
  • Offline mode listing what remains available, not just that you are offline
  • Conflict presented as two versions with authors and times, resolved by a human
  • Restricted with a remedy, and a configuration for the case where existence itself is sensitive
  • No fake progress. A determinate bar appears only when real progress is known
Interaction model

Retry is always available and never automatic more than twice — an endlessly self-retrying panel hides a real outage. Conflict resolution requires an explicit choice and records it.

Responsive behaviour

Identical everywhere. The stale indicator never collapses on a small screen; it is the one that matters most there, because mobile connections are the ones that drop.

Accessibility

State changes are announced once, politely. A loading region is aria-busy, not empty. Stale is in the region's accessible name so it is heard before the data.

Technical architecture

A wrapper with an error boundary and a suspense boundary, plus a staleness clock driven by the data's own timestamp rather than by fetch time. Composes with the shipping loaders — determinate progress uses InfusionLoader, indeterminate waits use the paced ones.

FHIR

Reads meta.lastUpdated for staleness and List.emptyReason for the semantic empty case, which is the FHIR element built for exactly this and almost never used.

11 · API design

The complexity goes underneath. The surface stays boring.

Three rules hold across all fifty. The FHIR resource is accepted directly where a sensible mapping exists, so the common case has no adapter. Every stateful component is controllable and uncontrolled, with the uncontrolled path being the one in the README. And the safety-relevant props are required — if leaving something out would produce a component that renders perfectly and says something false, it is not optional.

<ChartHeader patient={patient} encounter={encounter} />

<Trend series={weights} />

/>

Worker boundary

Three things move off the main thread: grid sort/filter/group above 2,000 rows, chart downsampling, and interaction-set computation in InteractionMatrix, which is O(n²) in the medication count and runs on every list change.

Network

Every fetching component takes an AbortSignal and cancels on patient change. This is not a performance nicety: a response that arrives after the chart has switched is a wrong-patient event.

Memory

Multi-chart workspaces cap retained charts at a configurable count and evict least-recently-used, keeping only identity and draft state. A clinician with fourteen tabs open is the normal case, not the edge.

SSR and hydration

Everything renders on the server except the four components that require media or window APIs, which export an explicit .Client entry rather than failing at hydration.

Mobile

Budgets above are for a mid-range Android on a hospital guest network, not a workstation. Grid row height and chart point density both drop at that tier — a fact the components read from a capability context.

Measured, not asserted

Each budget becomes a CI gate: size-limit for bytes, a Playwright trace assertion for frame cost on a 10,000-row fixture. A regression fails the build, as the contrast gate does today.

2.5.8 Target size (minimum)

24×24 CSS pixels for every control, including grid row affordances and timeline nodes. Compact density reduces spacing, never target size — which is the change most density implementations get wrong.

2.5.7 Dragging movements

Every drag has a non-drag equivalent: reordering a patient stack, reparenting a plan objective, resizing a dock, brushing a chart range. This is why the tablet plan column view exists at all.

3.3.7 Redundant entry

Intake and assessment flows carry answers forward within a session. In behavioral health this is also a clinical requirement — asking a trauma history twice in one visit is a harm, not an inconvenience.

Status glyphs, chart series, reference bands and focus rings all clear 3:1. Gated in the token build across three themes and every brand, as the repository already does for 21 token pairs.

prefers-reduced-motion gets a designed still state, not a paused one — the rule the loaders already follow. A streaming response becomes a progressive block reveal, not a frozen cursor.

The one that is not a checkbox

Critical clinical information must never depend on colour, and the honest test is not an audit tool — it is rendering the screen in greyscale and asking a clinician to triage from it. That test is cheap, it is repeatable, and it is the one this library should run on every component before release.

Licensing hazard we do not own

Several instruments are free (PHQ-9, GAD-7, AUDIT, C-SSRS for healthcare use); several are not (MMSE, MoCA and others require licences or certification). No component computes a clinical recommendation. Scoring an instrument by its published algorithm is arithmetic; suggesting a diagnosis or a dose is a medical-device question. That boundary is already the stated position on the marketplace and should hold here.

Six questions only you can answer

Question 01 · Blocking wave two

Who is the named clinician, and at what rate?

Eleven behavioral-health components encode clinical judgement. Without a named licensed reviewer — ideally one psychiatrist or PMHNP and one LCSW — the differentiator does not exist and we should not claim it. This is the same unanswered question as the marketplace brief. It is now blocking twice.

Question 02

Do we ship instrument content, or only the engine?

PHQ-9, GAD-7, AUDIT-C and C-SSRS can be embedded without a licence for healthcare use. Others cannot, and C-SSRS electronic implementations require per-version certification for commercial use. Shipping the engine only is safe and much less useful. My recommendation: ship the four free instruments as data with the licence recorded on each, and provide an authoring path for everything else.

Question 03

Is the Enterprise tier a product or a services artefact?

The eight application-level components are each two to six weeks. Sold as licensed components they invite support obligations we cannot staff. Sold as what a services engagement produces, they raise contract value and stay maintainable. The plan assumes the latter; the pricing table currently implies the former.

Question 04

Which regulatory surface do we assert, and which do we merely support?

ModelTransparencyCard renders the 31 HTI-1 attributes. That is materially different from claiming the component makes a customer's product compliant. I have written it as renders the required attributes throughout. Confirm we never soften that, including in sales material.

Question 05

Do we build for one EHR's app platform first?

SMART on FHIR makes these embeddable in Epic and Oracle Health as apps. That is a large distribution channel and a large amount of platform-specific work. Doing it is a strategy; doing it accidentally is a tax. My recommendation: stay platform-neutral through wave four, then decide with evidence.

Question 06

Scope of the first paid pack.

My recommendation is the behavioral-health pack from wave two, priced as a vertical pack rather than per-component, on the argument that it is the only set with no substitute. The alternative — leading with the grid and visualisation pack — is easier to demo and much easier to copy.

One thing I would argue about

Eleven behavioral-health components is a bet that the buyer is a behavioral-health platform. If the buyer turns out to be general digital health, the correct list is closer to six behavioral-health components and five more in operations and revenue. That is the single assumption in this document with the largest downstream consequence, and it is worth settling before wave two rather than during it.
