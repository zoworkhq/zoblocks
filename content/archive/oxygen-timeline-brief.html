01 — Thesis

A timeline is a claim about completeness, and almost every one on screen is false.

Everything else in this brief follows from one observation about how people read chronological lists.

A table is read as a set of rows. A chart is read as a shape. A timeline is read as an account — and an account is understood to be continuous. Nothing between two entries means nothing happened between them. Nothing before the last entry means the history starts there. This is not a mistake readers make carelessly; it is what the form promises.

Which is a problem, because the timeline on screen is nearly always a slice. It is paginated to five. It is filtered to one department. It is assembled from three source systems, one of which timed out four seconds ago and returned nothing. Every one of those produces the same rendering: a clean, confident, continuous list.

The failure this component exists to prevent

A clinician opens a chart, reads a timeline with no imaging on it, and orders a CT. The study was done eleven weeks ago at another hospital. The regional exchange query failed and the component rendered the events it had. Nothing was wrong on screen. The patient got a second dose of contrast and radiation for a scan that already existed.

This is not hypothetical framing. Missing information is a measured, routine feature of clinical work: about 13.6% of primary-care consultations proceed with clinical information missing, adversely affecting care in roughly half of them, and one emergency study found records missing or incomplete on admission for 27% of patients, with unnecessary procedures — including lumbar punctures — performed on 5% as a consequence. The literature's own term for the deeper problem is informative missingness: a NULL that gets read as a negative.

Three rules, each enforced by something that fails

01
The timeline states its own boundaries, or it does not compile.
coverage is a required prop. It names the window, the sources and their health, the applied filters, the sort direction, and how many events exist versus how many are shown. It renders as a sentence in a fixed place, it is never collapsed behind a tooltip, and it is one of the few things that always prints. A timeline whose boundaries you cannot describe is a timeline you may not render.
02
Planned is not happened.
FHIR already draws this line — Appointment records a planned activity, Encounter records an actual one — and every timeline in the field erases it by putting both in one stream. A future event renders on the other side of a now marker derived from a caller-supplied now, never the wall clock. And a planned event whose time has passed with nothing recorded against it is its own state: lapsed. That state is how a no-show becomes visible instead of becoming silence.
03
Two registers, never one weight.
An SMS reminder and a creatinine of 3.2 are not the same kind of fact. Every event carries a register — clinical, administrative, communication, patient-reported, system — and the component ranks, groups or splits by it. Undifferentiated is the one layout it will not produce, because that is the layout in which sixty automated reminders bury one encounter note.

Two secondary rules inherit from decisions this repository has already made, and are restated here only because a timeline is where they bite hardest. A time is rendered at the precision the record holds it — a FHIR 2019 stays 2019 and is never widened to 1 January. And a relative time never appears alone: "3 hours ago" beside a dose is not a clinical timestamp.

02 — The supplied design

Five things it gets right, and eighteen it cannot survive

Redrawn below in real Ant Design v6 token values so the judgement is about the structure and not about a screenshot's colour profile. The pins mark findings that are visually locatable; the rest are structural and listed after.

What it gets right, and should be kept

Keep

One rail, one column

No alternation. Alternating timelines double the horizontal scan distance, and they are the first thing to break at 320 px. This is the correct default and antd's own default (mode resolving to start) agrees.

Keep

The type has its own place

A 32 px ringed icon node gives the event's kind somewhere to live that is not the title. That is exactly the slot a text kind-label needs, which is the fix for finding 8.

Keep

What, then when

Title first, timestamp subordinate underneath. Most clinical timelines lead with the date, which optimises for a scan nobody performs — you look for the event, then check when.

Keep

The card admits it is a window

"See All" concedes that more exists. Most designs do not concede even that much. It is the right instinct expressed in the wrong grammar — see finding 1.

Keep

The rail runs off the edge

The clipped fifth entry and the continuing line are an honest gesture toward continuation. Worth keeping deliberately rather than as a side-effect of a fixed card height.

The eighteen

Findings 1–7 are pinned on the mockup. The rest are absences — things the structure has no slot for, which is why they are invisible until the day they matter.

01
"See All" is a link where a sentence is needed
Five of how many? Of twelve, this card is a summary. Of fourteen hundred, it is a rounding error presented as a history. And the reader cannot tell which, so they assume the first. Fix: Showing 5 of 43 · newest first stated in words, with "See all 43" as the control beside it.
02
Type is carried by an icon and a hue, and nothing else
The stethoscope ring is the only thing separating a clinical encounter from an email. Forced-colors mode flattens the ring, a monochrome print flattens it, and the icon has no text equivalent anywhere in the accessibility tree. This is the library's oldest rule — never colour alone — failing on the most repeated element on the screen.
03
"Reminder Email to Dr. Wade warren"
The reminder went to the doctor? Every line in this design names the same person, in a different role each time — performer, reviewer, recipient — and the structure has one slot for all three. Fix: actor, recipient and subject as separate fields, with the component composing the sentence. Also: warren, lowercase. A name assembled by concatenation rather than rendered from the record's own parts. formatHumanName() already exists in @oxygenui-design/fhir.
04
A date with no time, no zone, and no precision
Three problems wearing one hat. No time: two calls on 22 March are indistinguishable and their order is unverifiable. No zone: a transfer, a traveller or a teleconsult renders in the reader's zone, not the record's. No precision: a checkup known to the day and a history item known only as "2019" would format identically, so the component has to invent a day for the second one. Every one of these is a fact the record did not assert.
05
Three events compressed into one sentence
"form sent and response received and reviewed" is three things that happened on three days, flattened into prose dated to one of them. The reader cannot ask how long the patient took to respond, or how long the form sat unreviewed — which is the only question anyone actually asks of an intake form. Fix: a thread — one node that carries its own ordered steps, each with its own time and its own open/closed state.
06
Nothing here can have failed
A call that went to voicemail, an appointment that was cancelled, a form sent and never returned, an email that bounced — all four render identically to a call that connected. The design has states for things that happened and no state for things that were attempted. In a practice-management timeline that is most of the content.
07
The card ends, and the reader does not know why
Because the height ran out? Because the list did? Because a filter is applied elsewhere on the page? A rail running off the bottom edge is an honest gesture only when a sentence underneath says what it means.
08
Two registers at one weight
Four of the five entries are administrative. One is clinical. At five entries that reads as a rich account of a practice's attentiveness; at two hundred, sixty automated reminders bury the encounter note, and the clinician stops reading the timeline entirely — which is the actual observed failure mode of this pattern in the field.
09
The event type is fused into the title prose
"Routine Checkup: Dr. Wade warren" is a sentence a template built, not a record. It cannot be filtered, cannot be counted, cannot be translated without re-authoring, and cannot be read by anything but a human. kind + title + detail as three fields, composed by the component, gives all four back.
10
Nothing states the sort direction
It is newest-first. Reverse it and the identical component tells a completely different story with no visible difference except the numbers. Direction is not a preference to be inferred from two dates the reader happens to compare; it is part of the claim.
11
Planned and past share one stream
"Appointment: scheduled with…" is dated 24 March and sits between two things that happened. Was it scheduled on the 24th, or scheduled for the 24th? The structure cannot say, and there is no now anywhere on the surface to anchor against.
12
Nothing can be entered in error
FHIR is explicit: an erroneous record is retained and marked, not deleted — the entered-in-error state exists precisely so the correction is itself part of the record. This design has no way to draw a struck entry, so an implementer will filter it out, which is the one behaviour the standard forbids. The same gap covers amended and corrected.
13
No source, so a failed source is invisible
Records from this practice and records from a regional exchange look identical, and a source that returned nothing looks like a source with nothing to return. This is the failure in the box at the top of this document, and it is the single most consequential absence in the list.
14
No access model, so restricted events vanish
A psychotherapy note, a sealed adolescent record, a break-glass-only entry. With no way to render "this exists and you may not read it", the implementation filters them, and the timeline silently misrepresents the record to a clinician who has no way to know they are reading a redacted version. ChartAccordion already solved this shape; the AccessDescriptor type is reusable as-is.
15
Occurred and recorded are the same field
A note written six days after the visit it describes is a different document, evidentially, from one written that afternoon — and every retrospective chart review turns on that gap. One date field cannot hold it.
16
One empty state where there are at least three
No record at all; no events in this window; and no events you may see. Rendering all three as an empty card is the exact ambiguity "absence is a state" exists to prevent, and it is the one that produces a repeat CT.
17
It does not survive its own success
At forty events there is no grouping, so "what happened in June" is a manual scan. At four hundred — one year of a diabetic patient's observations — it is a scroll with no bottom. Grouping, clustering and a jump control are not enhancements here; they are the difference between the component working at demo scale and at chart scale.
18
The rail carries a meaning nobody authored
Not yet, in this drawing — but it will the moment it is built on Ant Design's own Timeline, which attaches a dotted rail segment to the last rendered item for reasons that have nothing to do with the data. Section 4 measures that from the source.
The shape of the fix

Fourteen of these eighteen are the same finding wearing different clothes: the model is a rendering, not a record. A title string, an icon, and a date is what you build when the events arrive pre-formatted from a template. Give each event a typed kind, a register, a status, an actor, a source, and two separate times, and eleven of the eighteen close without any visual change at all.

03 — Six real reads

What people are actually doing when they look at one of these

Nobody reads a timeline. They interrogate it, with a specific question, under time pressure. Six questions, and what each one demands of the component.

Read 1 · 90 seconds

"What has been happening to this person?"

A covering clinician picking up an unfamiliar chart before walking into the room. They read the top eight entries and stop.

Demands: newest-first; clinical register legible above administrative noise; the coverage sentence visible without scrolling, because those eight entries are about to become their whole mental model.

Read 2 · the dangerous one

"Has she had this before?"

Looking for a specific prior event — an imaging study, an allergy reaction, a previous admission — possibly years back.

Demands: filter by kind, jump to date, and above all an unambiguous account of what was searched. This is the read where not finding it is a clinical answer, and the component must never let "absent from this view" pass as "absent from the record".

Read 3 · the back office

"Did anyone actually call her?"

A practice manager auditing outreach after a missed appointment. This is what the supplied design is genuinely for, and it is a real job.

Demands: the administrative and communication registers promoted, not buried; attempts distinguishable from contacts; threads that show a form sent on the 12th and still unreturned on the 25th as one open item rather than one closed sentence.

Read 4 · adversarial

"Reconstruct what was known, and when."

A clinical reviewer, a complaint, a claim. The artefact they are building is literally called a chronology.

Demands: occurred and recorded as separate, both visible; entries entered in error retained and struck rather than removed; amendments showing what changed; source attribution per event; and a print that carries all of it, because this read ends as a PDF in a bundle.

Read 5 · the other audience

"What did my results say?"

The patient, in their own portal, at 11pm. Since April 2021 the information-blocking rule means results reach them at the same moment as the ordering clinician — frequently before anyone has called.

Demands: a separate string catalog, not a softened tone; a result that has not yet been reviewed saying exactly that; and no clinician shorthand anywhere on the surface.

Read 6 · moving target

"…and one more just arrived."

A result lands while the timeline is open, on a screen someone is mid-scroll through.

Demands: new items announced as content rather than as a count; nothing reordering under the reader's cursor; and a "new since you opened this" divider that survives a re-render, so the reader can tell what moved.

What reads 2 and 4 have in common

Both are reads where the absence of an entry is the finding. Those two reads are the entire justification for making coverage a required prop rather than an optional nicety, and they are why the coverage sentence prints. Reads 1, 3, 5 and 6 would be adequately served by a good-looking list.

04 — antd v6, measured

Ant Design ships a Timeline. I read it rather than assuming.

Measured against antd@6.6.0 and @rc-component/steps@1.2.2 as installed in this workspace — the source, not the documentation site, because the two disagree in at least one place.

What it actually is

antd/es/timeline/Timeline.js is a thin adapter over Steps. It imports Steps, renders it with type="dot", and swaps the element names through an internal context: 

// antd/es/timeline/Timeline.js — the whole mechanism, condensed
const stepInternalContext = { rootComponent: 'ol', itemComponent: 'li' };

<InternalContext.Provider value={stepInternalContext}>
  <UnstableContext.Provider value={{ railFollowPrevStatus: reverse }}>
    <Steps
      type="dot"
      items={mergedItems}
      current={mergedItems.length - 1}   // ← hardcoded
      variant={variant}
      orientation={orientation}
    />
  </UnstableContext.Provider>
</InternalContext.Provider>

Finding A — it is an <ol> of <li>, which is good news

Ordered list, correct element, and the documentation site's summary that it renders a <ul> is wrong. A chronology is ordered; ol is the right call and we should keep it.

Finding B — current is hardcoded to the last item, and it has a visual consequence

Steps derives a status per item from current: index below it is finish, index equal to it is process, above is wait. With current = items.length - 1, every timeline's last rendered item is permanently in the "process" state — and antd's timeline stylesheet contains exactly one rule keyed on it: 

// antd/es/timeline/style/index.js
[varName('item-process-rail-line-style')]: 'dotted'

So the last item's rail segment is dotted. On a wizard, "the step you are on, and it continues" is exactly right. On a chronology it is a mark of incompleteness applied to whichever event happens to be last — and because reverse reverses the array before current is computed, on a newest-first clinical timeline the dotted segment lands on the oldest event in the chart. Nobody authored that sentence and it is being said on every screen.

Why this one matters beyond antd

It is the cleanest example of the argument for this whole component: a dotted line in a clinical chronology is read. A reader who has learned that dotted means "gap in the record" — a reading we are about to make true, deliberately, in section 6 — will read antd's decorative artefact as a data claim. Inheriting a visual language you did not author is how a component library injects meaning into a chart.

Finding C — zero accessibility, and no seam to add it

@rc-component/steps@1.2.2 emits no role, no aria-current, no aria-label — a grep for aria across its ES build returns nothing. There is no prop to name the list, so a page with a patient timeline and an audit timeline gives a screen-reader user two unnamed lists. This is consistent with what we already recorded about antd's stance on WCAG, and it is not a defect we can file around: naming the list is not a feature antd exposes.

Finding D — v6 renamed most of the item API

Anyone arriving from antd v5 has already been through this, and our parity table has to encode the v6 spelling, not the familiar one.

A discrepancy to pin down before we claim parity

The documentation table gives mode a default of end. The 6.6.0 source resolves an unset mode to 'start' — the fallback branch of modeList.includes(mode) ? mode : 'start'. One of the two is wrong. Our parity test must assert against the installed version's behaviour and record the finding, not copy the documentation table.

The decision under ADR 0010

The reviewer test is does it own a clinical concept, or is it a control? This component splits cleanly across that line, which is why it is two components and not one.

QuestionTimelineCareTimeline
Control, or clinical concept? Control. A rail, dots, and a list. Concept. A chronology with a coverage claim.
Under ADR 0010 Match antd's API exactly, take no dependency. Organism. Built on our Timeline, not antd's.
Is the extension expressible as a wrapper? No — we must not set current, and antd offers no way not to. No — nothing in it is antd.
Anything expensive to inherit? No. An <ol>, a rail, and a dot. No.
Would wrapping cost the copy-source channel? Yes — antd in every consumer of a primitive. Yes, transitively.
Parity table in the test suite? Required, per ADR 0010. n/a

So: an antd-compatible Timeline primitive that an existing antd form adopts by changing one import, and a CareTimeline organism above it. This is the same pairing the repository already ships as Accordion and ChartAccordion, for the same reason, and the precedent is worth following exactly.

Three declared divergences from antd

  1. We do not set current, and we expose no equivalent. A history has no current step. This frees the dotted rail, and section 6 reassigns it to a meaning worth having.
  2. An accessible name is required, not optional. aria-label or aria-labelledby, enforced by the contract suite. antd cannot do this; we can, and a page with two timelines needs it.
  3. reverse exists on the primitive for parity, and is not the mechanism on CareTimeline, which takes order and states the direction in words. reverse is a rendering trick; direction is part of the claim.

Each of the three goes in the component's limitations, which is where this repository records deliberate divergence — the same place the Switch records loading not disabling the control.

05 — Sixty-one ideas

Everything it could do, and the nineteen that make v1

Badged v1 for the beta surface, later for designed-but-deferred, host for things the consuming application owns and we must not take, and no for ideas that are attractive and wrong.

A · Structure and layout
01
Single rail, newest first v1
The default, and the only layout that holds at 320 px.
02
Group headers by month, quarter or year v1
Chosen by span, not hardcoded: a fourteen-month history groups by month, a fourteen-year one by year.
03
Sticky group header inside a scrolling pane v1
You should never scroll past the point where you no longer know what month you are in.
04
Group by encounter or episode instead of by date later
"Everything that belonged to the March admission." The correct primary grouping for inpatient; needs an episode model first.
05
mode="register" — clinical one side, everything else the other v1
antd's alternate alternates for decoration. Ours alternates on a fact, and collapses to one column with kind labels under 760 px.
06
Swimlanes — one lane per source or per register later
The medico-legal reviewer's view. Genuinely useful, genuinely a different component's worth of layout code.
07
Horizontal band for a single episode or medication course later
antd v6 added orientation="horizontal"; our primitive matches it, CareTimeline stays vertical in v1.
08
Compact density v1
22 px nodes, one line per event, kind label retained. Three densities already exist in the token pipeline.
09
Card variant with a stated limit v1
The supplied design, done honestly. limit={5} plus a coverage line and a real "See all 43".
10
Minimap / density strip beside a long history later
A 4 px column showing where the events cluster across ten years. Beautiful, and it needs the scroll container to be ours.
B · Time
11
Precision-preserving dates v1
FHIR 2019, 2019-04 and 2019-04-02T09:15:00+05:30 render as what they are. Never widened.
12
Occurred and recorded as separate fields v1
Two dates, one event. The gap between them is evidence.
13
Late-entry marker when the gap exceeds a threshold v1
"Recorded 6 days later." Caller sets the threshold; the default is stated, not hidden.
14
Zone rendered from the record, not the reader v1
And a marker when an event's zone differs from the timeline's, because that is a transfer or a traveller.
15
Relative time, never alone v1
"2 days ago" is a convenience beside an absolute time and a lie in place of one.
16
A now marker from a caller-supplied now v1
Deterministic, so the component is visually regression-testable. Reading the wall clock is a lint error in this repo.
17
"New since you last reviewed this" divider v1
Takes seenThrough as a prop, never computes it. The single most requested affordance in every EHR usability study I have read, and roughly nine lines of code.
18
Stable ordering for identical timestamps v1
Bulk-imported records share an instant to the second. Ties break on a documented key so two renders never disagree.
19
Duration events — a course, an admission, an infusion later
A period is not a point. It wants a band, not a dot, and that is layout work.
20
Elapsed-between annotation later
"11 weeks" printed on the rail between two events. Excellent for a chronology, noisy everywhere else.
21
Age-at-event beside the date later
"aged 62". Paediatrics wants it in months. calculateAge() already exists.
C · Completeness and safety — the reason this component exists
22
coverage as a required prop v1
The window, the sources, the filters, the order, the counts. Omitting it is a type error.
23
The coverage sentence, in a fixed place, always v1
Never a tooltip, never an icon, never behind a disclosure. Composed from the data, not written by the caller.
24
Per-source health, named v1
"Regional exchange — could not be reached." A source that failed is a fact about the timeline, not a toast that has already gone.
25
A failed source escalates to a blocking banner v1
Not a grey footnote. This is the CT case, and it is worth interrupting for.
26
Dotted rail means "records may be missing here" v1
The affordance antd wastes on the last item. Paired with a worded label on the rail, because a dotted line alone is colour-by-another-name.
27
Three distinct empty states v1
No record · none in this window · none you may see. Three sentences, never one shrug.
28
Filters state themselves and their cost v1
"2 hidden by filter" beside the filter, not only in the filter's own UI, which is usually off screen.
29
Entered-in-error retained and struck v1
The standard says retain and mark. Removing it is the one thing a compliant renderer may not do.
30
Amendments show what changed v1
"Amended 14 Aug — result value corrected." An amendment with no statement of what moved is a rumour.
31
A cluster may never swallow a critical event v1
Collapsing 40 vitals into one node is necessary; collapsing the one critical potassium into it is the defect that collapsing invents. Promotion out of a cluster is an invariant with a test.
D · Clinical intelligence
32
Five registers, typed v1
clinical · administrative · communication · patient-reported · system.
33
A closed kind vocabulary, with an escape that costs something v1
Twenty kinds; kind: "other" requires a typeLabel, so an unlabelled unknown is a build error rather than a blank chip.
34
Threads — one node, several steps, each with its own time v1
Form sent → returned → reviewed. This is the fix for finding 5, and it is what the supplied design was reaching for with prose.
35
Lapsed — planned, the time passed, nothing recorded v1
The state that makes a no-show visible. Derived from now, never written back.
36
Actor, recipient and subject as separate fields v1
The component composes the sentence, so "Reminder email to Dr Warren" cannot be authored by accident.
37
Severity paired with a status word, or neither v1
Lifted wholesale from ChartAccordion, where the type already enforces it.
38
Access states — withheld, consent-gated, break-glass v1
Reuses AccessDescriptor. Says that something exists without saying what it is.
39
Provenance chip — patient-reported vs clinician-recorded v1
"Patient reported" beside a symptom onset is not a hedge, it is the correct attribution.
40
Result values inline on the node later
Wants ResultValue, which is not built. The interpretation rules live there and must not be re-implemented here.
41
Any automatic summarisation of the history no
A component that decides which events matter is clinical decision support wearing a list's clothing. Ranking by declared register is as far as this goes.
E · Interaction
42
Filter by register and by kind v1
Controlled, with the applied filter visible in the coverage sentence.
43
"Load older records" as a real button v1
Not infinite scroll. A chronology with no bottom cannot be printed, cannot be finished, and cannot be reasoned about.
44
Jump to date v1
Read 2's affordance. Scrolls to the nearest group and announces where it landed.
45
Expand a cluster in place v1
Disclosure, not navigation. The reader keeps their position.
46
Each event links into the record v1
An href or an onSelect, never both silently. The title is the link target — the whole row being clickable steals every text selection.
47
Keyboard: it is a structure, not a widget v1
Tab reaches controls, arrows do not hijack reading. Browse mode is how screen-reader users read a list, and a roving tabindex would take it away.
48
role="feed" for the infinite case later
The APG's own pattern for this, and its example carries a note that it has not reached task-force consensus. Opt-in behind a prop, after a screen-reader matrix, never the default.
49
New arrivals announced as content v1
A polite live region containing the event's own words. An empty live region announces nothing, which is the classic way this is got wrong.
50
Never reorder under the reader v1
Arriving events queue behind a "3 new" control unless the reader is at the top.
51
Select a range to build a chronology later
Read 4's real workflow, and a whole feature. Worth doing; not worth doing badly in v1.
F · Data, integration and output
52
FHIR adapters for eleven resource types v1
Encounter, Appointment, Communication, DocumentReference, QuestionnaireResponse, Observation, Procedure, MedicationRequest, Immunization, Consent, Task. Pure functions, no fetching.
53
A Bundle in, a timeline out v1
toTimelineEvents(bundle) over a Patient/$everything response, with unmapped types surfaced as a count rather than dropped.
54
Unmapped resources are reported, not discarded v1
"6 resources of 2 types not shown." A silent drop in an adapter is a coverage lie one layer down.
55
Any network access, polling or subscription no
Same rule Consult holds. The component takes data; the application fetches it.
56
Print stylesheet that keeps the coverage statement v1
Print is where "See all" stops existing, so it is where the sentence matters most.
57
Export the visible chronology as CSV or PDF host
Ours to make possible, the application's to own — it has the audit trail and the letterhead.
58
Patient-facing string catalog v1
A separate @oxygenui-design/intl catalog, per the house rule. Not a softened tone: different words.
59
"Not yet reviewed by your care team" on a patient-facing result v1
The honest rendering of the immediate-release rule. It is a fact about the record, and it is the difference between a portal that informs and one that ambushes.
60
Emit an AuditEvent when the timeline is read host
An onRead callback with the coverage it rendered; the application writes it. A component that writes audit records is infrastructure.
61
Custom-element channel later
The loaders and the Switch ship one. This has too much layout to be cheap in a shadow root, and no demand yet.
The cut

Nineteen features carry the beta: the required coverage with its sentence, banner and three empty states; register and kind typing; threads; the planned / now / lapsed axis; entered-in-error and amendments; access states; grouping with sticky headers; clustering with the critical-promotion invariant; precision-preserving time with occurred/recorded; the since-last-review divider; card, default, compact and register layouts; filter, jump, load older; the FHIR adapters; print; and the patient catalog. Everything badged later is designed enough to be sure it fits, and deliberately not built.

06 — The design

The default read, in real Ant Design v6 tokens

One synthetic patient — Ada Lovelace, 38, fourteen months of records across two source systems, one of which is down. Rendered at antd's default density with now = 18 Aug 2026 10:40 +05:30 supplied by the caller.

Seven decisions inside that frame

01
The rail is a grid column, not an absolutely positioned line
A 32 px column with a 1 px child that stretches. It survives a four-line title, 200% zoom, and RTL without a single physical offset. Every timeline that positions its rail absolutely breaks on the first long title.
02
Dashed border = planned. Dashed rail = gap. Both carry words.
Two different dashes, two different meanings, and neither is load-bearing on its own: "Planned" is a chip, and the gap has a sentence on the rail. Forced-colors keeps both.
03
The kind label sits above the title, in text
The slot the reference design left for the icon, filled with something a screen reader and a monochrome printer can both use. The icon becomes what it should have been: redundant.
04
Lapsed says what it does not know
"Scheduled, and no encounter has been recorded against it" — not "missed", not "no-show". The record cannot support either word, and the component may not invent them.
05
The amendment states the previous value
9.4% → 8.9%. An amendment chip with no delta tells the reader something changed and denies them the one thing they need.
06
The cluster carries its own safety claim
"None critical" is not reassurance copy; it is the invariant, rendered. A cluster containing a critical value cannot render that chip, because the critical event is promoted out of the cluster before it forms.
07
The restricted event is a sentence, not a redaction bar
Black boxes read as a design flourish. A line of text reads as a fact, and it is the fact that matters: something is here, and this reader is not seeing it.

08 — The event model

Fourteen fields, and three of them are the whole product

register, status and coverage. Everything else in this type exists in some form in every timeline component ever written. 

/**
 * One thing that happened, or was planned, or was attempted.
 *
 * Times are strings, not Dates. A FHIR `2019` means 2019 — parsing it into a
 * Date invents a January, a first, a midnight, and a time zone, none of which
 * anybody recorded.
 */
export interface TimelineEvent {
  id: string;

  /** What sort of thing it is. Closed vocabulary — see KINDS below. */
  kind: TimelineKind;
  /** Required when kind is "other". An unlabelled unknown is a build error. */
  typeLabel?: string;

  /** Which stream it belongs to. Drives ranking, grouping and layout="register". */
  register: "clinical" | "administrative" | "communication"
          | "patient-reported" | "system";

  /** When it happened, at the precision the record holds. */
  occurred: FhirDateTime;
  /** When it entered the record, if that is a different fact. */
  recorded?: FhirDateTime;

  /** What happened. A noun phrase — never a sentence with the actor in it. */
  title: React.ReactNode;
  /** One more line, when the title alone would be cryptic. */
  detail?: React.ReactNode;

  status: TimelineStatus;
  /** Present only for amended | corrected | in-error. Says what moved. */
  revision?: { at: FhirDateTime; by?: Party; was?: string; reason?: string };

  /** Who did it, who it was to, whose record it is. Three roles, three fields. */
  actor?: Party;
  recipient?: Party;
  subject?: Party;

  /** Which system this came from. Renders, and feeds the coverage statement. */
  source?: string;

  /** Exists, and you may not read it. Same shape ChartAccordion already uses. */
  access?: AccessDescriptor;

  /** A workflow with its own internal times. The fix for "sent and received and reviewed". */
  steps?: readonly TimelineStep[];

  /** Where this event lives in the record. */
  href?: string;
}

/**
 * Severity travels with a status word or not at all — lifted from ChartAccordion,
 * where a coloured rail with nothing beside it is already a type error.
 */
export type TimelineEventProps =
  | (TimelineEvent & { severity: Severity; severityStatus: React.ReactNode })
  | (TimelineEvent & { severity?: undefined });

Twenty kind values, grouped by register: clinical — encounter, observation, laboratory, imaging, procedure, medication, immunization, condition, allergy, note, document, care-plan; administrative — appointment, referral, task, coverage, consent; communication — message, call; system — access. Plus other, which costs a typeLabel.

Coverage — the type that makes the claim

/**
 * What this timeline is a view of. Required. There is no default, because
 * every plausible default is a claim the caller did not make.
 */
export interface TimelineCoverage {
  /** The period searched. Omitted `from` means "everything held". */
  window?: { from?: FhirDateTime; to?: FhirDateTime };

  /** Every system asked, and how it answered. An empty array is a type error. */
  sources: readonly [TimelineSource, ...TimelineSource[]];

  /** How many events match the query, if the server said. */
  total?: number;
  /** What is hidden, and why. Rendered beside the count, not inside a filter panel. */
  hidden?: readonly { reason: "filter" | "page" | "access" | "unmapped"; count: number; label?: string }[];

  order: "newest-first" | "oldest-first";
}

export interface TimelineSource {
  id: string;
  label: string;
  /** "unavailable" is the state this whole component was built for. */
  status: "ok" | "partial" | "unavailable" | "excluded";
  /** Required when status is not "ok" — a failure with no reason is a shrug. */
  detail?: string;
  /** How far back this source was able to answer for. */
  window?: { from?: FhirDateTime; to?: FhirDateTime };
}
Why sources is a non-empty tuple

readonly [T, ...T[]] rather than readonly T[]. An empty array would let a caller satisfy the type while claiming nothing, which is exactly the state the prop exists to forbid. A timeline assembled from one system says so; that is one line, and it is true.

09 — Accessibility

A structure, not a widget — which is the harder discipline

The temptation with a long list is to make it operable. Every arrow key you claim is a reading key you take away, and a screen-reader user reads a list in browse mode.

Elements and roles

  • <ol> of <li>, one list per group. A chronology is ordered and the order carries meaning; ul would be a lie the markup tells. antd v6 already gets this right and we keep it.
  • role="list" stated explicitly. Safari drops list semantics from any list with list-style: none, and VoiceOver then announces neither the list nor its item count. The redundant role is the accepted fix and the HTML validator's complaint about it is wrong here. It goes in with a comment naming the bug, so nobody "cleans it up".
  • An accessible name is required. aria-label or aria-labelledby, enforced by a test, because a chart with a care timeline and an access-history timeline otherwise presents two unnamed lists.
  • Each group is a <section> labelled by its heading, so the month headers are landmarks a rotor can jump between. This is what replaces the arrow keys we are not claiming.
  • No role="feed" in v1. It is the APG's pattern for exactly this and its own example carries a note that it has not reached task-force consensus; support is uneven. It stays behind a prop, after a screen-reader matrix, and never as the default.

What each event announces

In order, and nothing that is only visual: kind, then any status word, then the title, then the detail, then who, then the absolute time. The relative time is aria-hidden — "two days ago" adds nothing to a spoken absolute date and doubles the length of every item.

Forced colours

Every state survives the palette being taken away

Because no state is carried by colour in the first place: planned is a dashed border and a chip that says Planned; a gap is a dashed rail and a sentence; entered-in-error is a strike-through and a chip and a paragraph. Tested in three engines, as with every other component here.

Reduced motion

A designed still state, not a paused one

The only motion in the component is a cluster expanding and a new event arriving. Under prefers-reduced-motion both are instant, and the new-arrival highlight becomes a persistent left border rather than a fade — a fade that has finished is a state the reader never saw.

320 px and 200%

The rail is a grid column, so it just narrows

layout="register" collapses to one column under 760 px. Group headers stay sticky. Nothing scrolls horizontally except a cluster's own contents, in its own container.

Live regions

Announce the event, not the number

A polite region containing the arriving event's kind, title and time. "3 new items" is a notification; the point of a live region here is that a critical result announces itself without the reader going to look.

The one I am least sure of

Whether the group headings should be h3 or a role="heading" at a caller-supplied level. ChartAccordion takes headingLevel and that is the right precedent, but a timeline nested inside a tab panel inside a chart page can end up four levels deep, and getting it wrong produces a heading outline that is worse than no headings. It takes the prop, it defaults to nothing, and it renders a labelled section rather than a heading when no level is given.

11 — Rules that bind

Three that change the design, and one claim we do not make

Not a compliance section. These are the places where an external rule decides a rendering question that would otherwise be taste.

01
An erroneous record is retained and marked, never removed
FHIR's own lifecycle guidance is explicit: once an erroneous record exists, in clinical settings it must be retained and flagged as created in error, because the correction is itself part of the record. The common implementation — filter entered-in-error out of the query — is the reading that fails read 4 in section 3, and it fails it silently. Consequence for the design: in-error is a rendered state with a strike, a chip and a revision box, and the component has no prop that hides it.
02
Results reach the patient at the same moment they reach the clinician
The ONC information-blocking rule has applied to providers since 5 April 2021, and the practical effect is that a patient can read a result in a portal before the ordering clinician has opened it. The design question this settles: a patient-facing timeline must be able to say "released to you, not yet reviewed by your care team" — because the alternative is a portal that shows a number with no context and no indication that anyone is coming. Consequence: audience="patient" is a v1 feature, not a v2 nicety, and it selects a different string catalog rather than a softer tone.
03
Planned and actual are different resources, and the difference is normative
Appointment records a planned activity; Encounter records what actually occurred. A timeline that renders both as "things on the record" erases a distinction the data model went to trouble to make. Consequence: the now divider, the lapsed state, and the rule that a lapsed appointment says what is not known rather than asserting a no-show.
The claim we do not make

Rendering a complete-looking timeline is not a statement that the record is complete, and coverage is not a completeness guarantee — it is an accurate report of what this query reached. If the source system itself is missing records, the component will faithfully report that it reached a source which returned nothing to report. That limitation is stated in meta.limitations in those words, because a safety control that is trusted beyond its actual guarantee is worse than none.

One engine, two components, no new packages

The whole thing fits inside the registry channel, which keeps it installable as source and keeps antd out of every consumer.

13 — Testing

Assert the safety claim, not the render

Every failure in section 2 looks fine on screen. A suite that checks the component renders would pass on all eighteen.

The eleven that are the point

// 1 — the claim the whole component exists to make
it("names a source that could not be reached, above the fold", ...);
it("never renders an empty timeline without saying which kind of empty", ...);
it("states the applied filter and the count it hides", ...);
it("prints the coverage sentence", ...);        // asserted on the print stylesheet

// 2 — the record's own rules
it("renders an entered-in-error event, struck and marked, and never removes it", ...);
it("shows what an amendment changed, not only that it changed", ...);
it("says an event exists that the reader may not open, without saying what it is", ...);

// 3 — the invariants that clustering and sorting can break silently
it("promotes a critical event out of a cluster", ...);
it("loses no event across sort, group and cluster", ...);   // property test
it("orders identical timestamps identically on every render", ...);
it("keeps a year-only date a year", ...);
Property tests

The pipeline may not lose an event

Generate n events with mixed precisions, statuses and sources; assert that rendered + clustered + hidden = n for every combination of layout, group and filter. Sort-then-group-then-cluster is three transformations and this is the one thing all three can break without a visible symptom.

Parity

An antd rename fails our build, not a customer's

timeline-parity.test.ts reads antd's own TimelineProps and TimelineItemType from the installed package and asserts our surface matches, deprecations included. Modelled on the existing switch-parity.test.ts. It also pins the mode default discrepancy from section 4 to whichever value the installed version actually produces.

Determinism

Nothing reads the wall clock

now is a prop and a lint rule already forbids the alternative. That is what makes the now divider, the lapsed state and the relative times visually regression-testable at all — every VRT baseline is taken at a frozen instant.

Story-derived

Eighteen states, eighteen stories, no exceptions

pnpm gen --strict fails a beta component whose declared state has no story, and axe runs per story × themes × densities. The declared-state list in meta.ts is therefore a test manifest, not documentation.

The test I would write first

it("never lets a hidden event become an invisible one"): render with a filter, a page limit, an access restriction and a failed source all at once — the realistic case, and the one no demo shows — and assert that the rendered text accounts for every one of the 43 events in words. It is the single assertion that, if it holds, means the component has done its job.

Hand-written, because it cannot be derived

01
ADR 0011 — a component that summarises a set declares the set's boundaries
This is an architecture decision, not a component one: the same rule will bind the worklist, the results list, and the medication list when they are built. Writing it as an ADR now means the next component inherits the argument instead of re-deriving it — and it is the record of why a required prop was worth annoying people over.
02
A migration note for antd Timeline users
The six v5→v6 renames, our three declared divergences, and — stated plainly — that we do not reproduce the dotted last-item rail, with the reason. Someone will notice the visual difference and file it as a bug; this note is the answer.
03
A page on reading a timeline safely
Under content/, addressed to the implementer rather than the clinician: what coverage can and cannot promise, how to populate it from a real multi-source query, and why a timed-out source has to reach the component rather than being swallowed by the data layer. The most common way to defeat this component is to catch the error upstream.
04
A CONTENT.md addition — the coverage sentence's grammar
The sentence is composed, not authored, so its templates are clinical copy and belong under the existing content rules: absence is stated rather than punctuated, and both catalogs — clinician and patient — get their own phrasings rather than one tone-shifted string.
05
Prop tables — none, written by hand
Extracted from the types. A prop table is read as a contract and one that has silently drifted is worse than none. Listed here only so it is explicit that the omission is policy.

16 — Decide

Five decisions, and one thing I would argue about

Recommendations attached. If you say nothing, I will build the recommendation and record it here, as with the previous components.

01
The name — CareTimeline
Recommended. "Care" spans the registers the component actually carries; two of the five are not chart material at all. Runners-up and why they lost: ChartTimeline pairs beautifully with ChartAccordion but claims the events are all in the chart, which is the exact conflation finding 8 is about; PatientTimeline matches the reference label and reads as though it belongs to the identity family, which owns the Patient* prefix for a different purpose; Chronology is the real medico-legal instrument and is opaque to everyone who has not built one. The primitive is Timeline, which is not negotiable — it has to match antd's name to be a drop-in.
02
Is coverage genuinely required?
Recommended: yes. This is the decision that makes the component worth building, and it is the one that will generate complaints — every other timeline in the world takes an array and renders it. The escape hatch is honest and cheap: coverage={{ sources: [{ id: "app", label: "This application", status: "ok" }], order: "newest-first" }} is four lines and it is true. If you would rather it were optional with a warning, say so now, because half the design follows from this answer.
03
Free, or Pro?
Recommended: free, both items. Consistent with the calls already made — tier: "pro" keeps an item out of the public registry entirely, and the registry is the adoption engine. If anything here ever becomes Pro it should be the swimlane chronology view, which is a specialist workflow with a specialist buyer, not the timeline itself.
04
Do the FHIR adapters ship in v1?
Recommended: yes, phase 5. They are the reason to choose this over a styled list, and without them every evaluator writes their own mapping and gets entered-in-error wrong. The cost is four days and a real maintenance surface as FHIR resources evolve. The alternative — ship the event model in v1, adapters in v2 — is defensible and I do not prefer it.
05
Does audience="patient" belong on this component?
Recommended: yes, as a catalog switch. The repository's rule is that patient-facing and clinician-facing strings are different catalogs, not different tones, and a prop that selects the catalog honours that. The counter-argument is Consult's precedent, where a patient-facing product was ruled a separate package with a different name — but that was about a different surface and a different risk model. Here it is the same list of the same events with different words, and splitting the package would duplicate eighteen states to change a string table.

Ten things the build changed about this brief

Written after the fact, because a design document that quietly matches the code it produced is a document nobody re-read. Each of these was decided at the keyboard.

The brief saidThe code doesWhy
Coverage gaps are drawn where a source failed. coverage.gaps — declared by the caller, placed by the engine. Inferring "records may be missing" from eleven quiet weeks is the component inventing a claim. Placement runs over the whole ordered list rather than inside a group, because the interesting gaps are exactly the ones that span a month boundary.
Filtering is the host's job. registers / defaultRegisters / onRegistersChange, and the component folds the filter's cost into the sentence. A filter that hides five events and says so only inside its own popover — usually off screen — has not said so. Owning the filter is the only way to own that number.
Eleven FHIR adapters. Thirteen, in their own registry item (timeline-fhir). Task and Condition were already typed and cost four lines each. Separating the item keeps the FHIR dependency off anyone installing only the primitive.
The adapters read @oxygenui-design/fhir. They do — after seven resource interfaces were added to it. Encounter, Communication, DiagnosticReport, Procedure, Immunization, QuestionnaireResponse and Task did not exist. Each union is written out rather than typed as string, so entered-in-error is nameable.
Dates are formatted by @oxygenui-design/intl. formatFhirDateTime lives in the engine; the two catalogs are objects, like the accordion's. The intl package has no catalog yet, and inventing one for this component is the point at which changing a word becomes a breaking change. The limitation is restated honestly in meta.
"recorded 6 days after it happened". "recorded 18 Aug 2026". A date is a fact the record holds. A humanised duration needs a unit vocabulary in every catalog, and it is an adjective the record did not write.
— The props extractor learned Object.assign(Root, { Item }). Timeline documented zero props on merge — the same failure the extractor was written to end, in the compound-component shape antd uses. Fixed there rather than by contorting the export.
Primitive ≤ 6 KB; barrel unmentioned. Primitive 8 KB; barrel raised 38 → 45 KB. Timeline measures 7.17 KB and six of those are tailwind-merge reached through cn — the accordion's finding, again. The barrel raise is the third; .size-limit.md now says plainly that the number has stopped modelling anything.
role="feed" opt-in behind a prop. Not shipped in any form. A prop nobody can safely turn on is an API surface with no behaviour behind it. It goes in when the screen-reader matrix exists, and limitations says so.
— Four entries added to the docs app's hand-kept paths. The exact drift its own comment warns about, caught by typecheck rather than by a reader noticing a preview was missing.

What the review pass found

Every gate was green and the component was wrong in six places. Recorded here rather than quietly fixed, because the pattern in the first three is the same one and it is worth naming: a test that asserts a class name passes while the class does nothing.

01
layout="register" rendered a class and nothing else
The stylesheet targeted .ox-care-timeline__columns; the component never rendered one. So the promised two-column split was a no-op — and the test passed, because it asserted [data-ox-layout="register"] and the presence of the coverage sentence, both of which were true. The replacement asserts that an encounter is in the clinical column and a phone call is not, and that the split loses no rows.
02
The stylesheet was never imported by the docs site
Written, wired into the registry item, concatenated into the published package — and missing one @import in globals.css. Both pages shipped a chronology with no rail, no dashed gap and no column split. Nothing caught it: axe does not check styling and jsdom applies no CSS at all. Found by looking at the page. test/docs-stylesheets.test.ts now fails when a registry stylesheet is not loaded by the docs, not emitted into the React package, or has no owner in the class-namespace check — and adding that third assertion revealed that loader.css and switch.css had no owner either.
03
Two browser tests skipped on every run
Both guarded with test.skip when their fixture was absent, and the fixture was always absent because the docs page shows one scenario at a time. Playwright reports a skip as not-a-failure. This repository's own config carries a paragraph about exactly this. They now select the scenario; there is nothing to skip.
04
A year-only date was headed "January 2019"
The group key came from the value's lower bound, so a FHIR 2019 landed in a month bucket — inventing a January in exactly the place the component refuses to invent one on the event itself. A bucket may now never be finer than the record's own precision.
05
An ungrouped card was headed "All"
A heading that says nothing is still a heading in the outline a screen-reader user navigates by. Sections now carry titled, and the one produced by group="none" is not.
06
Four v1 features from §5 were simply not built
Jump-to-date (44), the arrivals live region (49), the kind filter (42), and the provenance attribution on patient-reported events (39). All four are in now, with tests. The live region announces the event's own words rather than a count, because "3 new items" is a notification and the point of a live region on a chart is that a result announces itself.
One thing still not fixed

e2e/signature.spec.ts's keyboard test flakes in Firefox under parallel load — it failed once in a full e2e:ci run and passed three times out of three in isolation. It is untouched by this work and pre-dates it, so it is reported rather than chased.

And one thing I would push back on, if you disagree

If the ask is to ship the reference card as drawn — five entries, "See All", no coverage line — I would build everything else and still refuse that one. Not because the card is bad; it is the right shape and layout="card" exists to serve it. But a five-item window onto an unstated whole, on a clinical surface, is the single defect this library was founded to make unwritable, and the fix costs one line of text at the bottom of a card. Every other recommendation in this document is negotiable. That one I would want to lose an argument about rather than concede quietly.
