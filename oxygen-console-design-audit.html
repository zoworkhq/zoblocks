Section 01

What is already right

Stated first so the plan does not re-solve it, and so the effort estimate is believable. None of the following needs revisiting.

Accessibility

verified

Zero WCAG 2.2 AA violations across 16 screens × 2 themes × 3 engines, plus no horizontal scroll at 320 px. This is measured on every run, not asserted.

e2e/console-a11y.spec.ts · 7 tests

The disabled-with-reason pattern

keep

Every blocked control stays focusable and carries the reason beneath it. It is applied consistently and it is genuinely better than the industry default of hiding the control.

components/ui/Button.tsx · lib/roles.ts whyNot()

Depth vocabulary

keep

Paper → surface → instrument, with the dark panel reserved for anything rendering something. It gives the product a signature and it is applied correctly.

app/globals.css · .surface, .instrument, .preview-region

Error copy

keep

Failures state the pair, the measured ratio, the floor and the WCAG criterion. Most products ship “Invalid colour”. Do not dilute this while shortening elsewhere.

Section 02

Authentication — the weakest surface

The first screen a client sees is the least designed one in the product. It is a centred card on an almost-invisible wash, floating in roughly 60% dead space, with no product proof and no social sign-in.

No social sign-in at all

high

Google and Microsoft are absent. For an enterprise console sold into health systems, Microsoft in particular is the expected path — its absence reads as unfinished rather than as a deliberate security posture.

app/(auth)/login/LoginForm.tsx

Password reset is built and unreachable

high

The password_resets collection, its TTL index and its token hashing all exist and are referenced in seven places. No screen links to them. A sign-in with no “forgot password” is the single most common support ticket in any product.

db/collections.ts PasswordResetDoc · lib/auth.ts

The card carries no proof

medium

A premium console earns trust before the credential field. Nothing on this screen says what the product does or that it is a clinical-grade system — the two facts that justify the price. Right-hand panel with a live token/contrast demonstration is the obvious move, and the material for it already exists.

app/(auth)/layout.tsx

Vertical composition is unresolved

medium

The card is top-aligned under a small header with pt-8 sm:pt-16, so at desktop heights it sits high with a large empty field beneath. It reads as a fragment of a page rather than a composition.

app/(auth)/layout.tsx

Required markers are noise

low

Every field on a two-field form is marked *. When everything is required, marking anything is decoration. Mark the optional ones instead, or nothing.

components/ui/Field.tsx

Recommended composition

Split screen at ≥1024 px: form left at a fixed 26 rem in an optically centred column; right pane an always-dark instrument showing a live contrast readout over the Northwind palette — the product demonstrating its own thesis. Below 1024 px the right pane drops and the form centres. Social buttons above the email field, separated by a labelled rule, because that is where a returning user looks first.

Section 03

Component consistency

The kit covers 17 components and the screens use it well. What breaks consistency is what the kit does not cover — every gap has been filled ad hoc, in more than one way.

GapCurrent stateSitesSeverity
No Select Raw <select> with a hand-copied class string; no focus ring beyond the global outline, no disabled treatment, no size parity with Input. 5 high
No Textarea Raw <textarea> with its own border and padding, visibly different from Input. 1 medium
No Toast Every action result renders inline inside its own form. On the theme page that means a publish confirmation appears in a 22 rem column in the header, where it is easy to miss. — high
DownloadButton re-implements buttonClasses A hand-written anchor class string that duplicates the secondary variant and will drift from it. 1 medium
Ad-hoc button padding className="px-2 py-1" and px-2.5 py-1.5 passed at call sites; there is no size prop, so “small” means four different things. 7 medium
Emoji beside a line-icon set 🔒 ⚠ ✓ ✗ used as glyphs while the rest of the product uses Lucide. Different weight, different metrics, different rendering per platform. 8 medium
Two empty-state treatments EmptyState on two screens; a bare <p class="surface"> in four DataTable empty props. 6 medium
One loading skeleton for every screen A single loading.tsx renders a table skeleton — shown even for the three-pane token editor, which looks nothing like a table. 1 medium
Semantic collision: StatusChip carries two unrelated meanings

The same component and the same tones express lifecycle (draft, published, live) and severity (passes, fails, clinical). A green chip therefore means both “this is live” and “this clears the contrast floor”, and on the history screen both appear in one row. Split into StatusChip (lifecycle, neutral shapes) and VerdictChip (severity, retains the pass/fail palette).

Section 04

Motion, and the absence of it

The design system defines a motion vocabulary that the product never uses. Nothing animates except colour, so every state change is instantaneous and slightly jarring.

/* globals.css — defined, and used exactly zero times */ .lift 0 usages translateY + shadow on hover --ease-spring 0 usages cubic-bezier(.34,1.56,.64,1) --ease-out-expo 2 usages mark rotation, rail transitions /* what has no transition at all */ Callout appears instantly incl. every save + publish result Skeleton no shimmer static grey blocks Panel / card no hover state framework cards are clickable Rail (mobile) no scrim, no slide menu snaps open

Results appear with no entrance

high

A publish confirmation, a validation failure and a save acknowledgement all pop into existence. This is the single highest-value motion fix: a 180 ms fade-and-rise on Callout makes the whole product feel considered, and it is one component.

components/ui/Callout.tsx · action-form.tsx

Skeletons do not read as loading

medium

Static blocks look like empty content. A slow shimmer — respecting prefers-reduced-motion — is the convention readers already understand.

components/ui/Skeleton.tsx

Table scroll has no affordance

medium

Wide tables scroll inside their container, correctly — but nothing indicates it. A masked edge fade that appears only when there is more to see solves it without a scrollbar.

Section 05

Layout, responsiveness and rhythm

The token editor collapses too late

high

Its three panes are gated at xl (1280 px), so on a 1280 px laptop — the most common desktop width there is — the editor renders as one stacked column and the live preview falls below the fold. It should split at lg, with the navigator collapsing first and the preview held.

tokens/TokenEditor.tsx · xl:grid-cols-[10rem_1fr_17rem]

Mobile menu has no scrim

medium

The rail expands in flow and pushes content down rather than overlaying it, so on a phone the page appears to jump. An overlay with a dimmed backdrop and a close affordance is the expected behaviour.

components/Rail.tsx

Content column is uniform regardless of content

low

Every screen sits in max-w-[900px]. The token editor and playground want more; settings and typography want less. One measure for a table and a form is a compromise that suits neither.

app/(app)/layout.tsx

Page headers are heavy

medium

Every screen opens with an eyebrow, a large title and a two-line lede — roughly 180 px before any content. On a return visit it is the same 180 px every time. Ledes should shorten to one line and the largest sizes reserved for the entry screens.

components/ui/PageHeader.tsx · all 13 screens

Section 06

Copy

The voice is genuinely good — specific, reasoned, never marketing. It is also long. The product explains itself at length on every screen, including ones a user visits daily.

ScreenCurrentRecommended
Frameworks Lede + two full explanatory paragraphs in a panel, ~140 words below the fold. Keep the clinical explanation, move it behind a “Why?” disclosure. One-line lede.
Members Lede, two callouts, plus a role-reference panel repeating what the table shows. Drop the reference panel; put role descriptions in the <select> option titles.
Token editor Three-sentence lede on the screen users spend the most time on. One sentence. The screen teaches itself through the contrast readouts.
Login “Author a design language, validate it against the same gate the build uses, publish it.” Good line — promote it to the marketing pane, not the form.

Section 09

Definition of done

Checkable, because “feels premium” is not something a build can verify and these are.

Consistency

  • Zero raw <select> or <textarea> outside the kit
  • Zero emoji used as an icon
  • Zero className padding overrides on Button
  • Every DataTable has an EmptyState

Experience

  • Every action result is announced and animated
  • Token editor keeps three panes at 1280 px
  • Mobile rail overlays rather than pushes
  • Login offers Google, Microsoft, email and reset

Held

  • axe stays at zero across 3 engines × 2 themes
  • No horizontal scroll at 320 px
  • All motion respects prefers-reduced-motion
  • VRT baselines for 8 screens, light and dark

Unchanged

  • Clinical tokens stay locked in every tier
  • Error copy keeps its measurements
  • Disabled controls keep their reason
  • Tenant isolation untouched
